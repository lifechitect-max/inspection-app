# Hacker Board verdict: the AI card business

*Round 3, the Chair's call. Date: 2026-10-01. Inputs: MISSION.md, the research report "AI card collecting business EV", 27 Round 1 proposals from 9 seats, 9 Round 2 ballots and the tally.*

## 1. The answer

**Conditional yes.** A card business is +EV for you only as a **collection-buying desk**, meaning you buy whole collections from non-collectors (heirs, downsizers, ex-collectors) below market and resell through low-fee channels. Holding inventory, flipping, grading for profit and running breaks are all -EV or pay about minimum wage. In the honest middle case the desk makes **about $3,500 a month profit before tax at month 12** (range $2,500 to $4,500), on about 18 to 20 hours a week and $10k to $12k of working capital. That is good but below your $5k to $10k goal. In the Chair's judgment there is roughly a 1-in-4 chance of clearing $5k a month by month 12. The downside is capped at about **$2,500** by a written 90-day kill rule.

## 2. The move

**The Collection Desk, upgraded.** This is business-2, which won the vote, with the best parts of seven other collection-buying variants folded in. Nine seats independently proposed the same profit pool. That agreement is the signal, so the Chair is not picking between versions. One machine takes the best piece from each.

**What it is:** Google Search ads and estate-sale partners send people with old cards to a "free photo estimate" page. Claude triages every submission within a few hours, and every qualified lead gets routed to one of four places, so even leads you don't want earn something:

| Route | When | What you earn |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy** | Clearly identified lots, cert-verifiable slabs, liquid value up to your per-deal cap | Pay about **50% of liquid value**, resell at about 85% net, so roughly 35 points of spread |
| **Consign** | Lots worth $3k+, raw vintage you can't safely judge yet, anything over your bankroll | 20 to 25% of net proceeds, with **no capital tied up** (leverage-1) |
| **Refer / overflow** | Out-of-radius or out-of-expertise qualified leads | Prepaid lead packs to vetted dealers (about $100 per photo-qualified seller), with the seller's consent on the form (first-principles-3, business seat) |
| **Decline kindly** | Junk wax (1987–94) and kids' modern binders | A free honest report. Bulk can go to a shop or into Q4 gift bundles (demand-2) |

**The hack that makes it work:**
1. **You buy seller attention that dealers don't.** Seller-intent clicks cost $1 to $5 (checked below), and almost no card dealer runs disciplined PPC. Your Amazon PPC skill is the unfair edge. The cards are not.
2. **AI removes the dealer's most expensive cost.** Sorting a shoebox to find the 20 cards that matter used to take an expert hours. Claude does it from photos in minutes, so you can say no to 80% of leads without driving anywhere.
3. **"The dealer who shows the receipts."** Every seller gets two offers side by side (cash now, or consign for more) plus a value band and the method behind it. The itemized card-by-card comps come after they accept, or on the consign path, so they can't shop your work to a mail-in buyer (first-principles dissent, adopted). Frame the cash price as a fee for speed and convenience, not a discount for what they don't know. The information gap shrinks every year as consumer AI apps improve. The hassle gap doesn't.
4. **Free supply that compounds.** Estate-sale companies and senior move managers get a 5% finder's fee plus a free report for their client (growth-1, arbitrage-2). Every report is a shareable link that heirs forward to siblings. Ask for a Google review at payout.
5. **Target heirs and vintage, not kids.** Broad "sell pokemon cards" traffic is mostly modern binders. Ad groups go after old, vintage, inherited, dad's or grandpa's cards, WOTC and 1st edition, with heavy negatives for modern set names, "app", "ebay" and "online" (marketing and demand seats).
6. **A novice-safe ladder** (mindset seat). For the first 60 days, buy outright only cert-verifiable slabs and clearly identified lots under $1,500, and consign or refer everything else. Raw vintage buys unlock after 10 logged deals with realized margin of 30% or more. A one-line decision journal for each offer (predicted vs. realized value) builds your calibration.

**Not in the core:** the vendor-prepaid monthly card show (business-1) is an **optional add-on from month 4**, and only if the desk passes its day-90 gate. It then becomes a free intake booth ("free collection appraisal at the door") and an exit for mid-tier cards.

### Spot-checks on the two claims that decide the vote

**Claim 1: seller-intent clicks cost $1 to $5. CONFIRMED, with one caveat.** The Chair re-pulled Google Keyword Planner via AdWhispr on 2026-10-01 (US, search):

| Keyword | Searches/mo | Top-of-page CPC |
|---|---|---|
| where to sell my baseball cards | 4,400 | $1.20–3.50 |
| where to sell baseball cards near me | 4,400 | $1.32–3.38 |
| best places to sell pokemon cards | 2,400 | $1.39–5.21 |
| best place to sell baseball cards | 1,600 | $1.53–4.11 |
| pokemon card buyer near me | 1,300 | $1.08–3.86 |
| selling my pokemon cards | 1,000 | $1.09–4.83 |
| sell my baseball cards | 390 | $1.32–3.64 |
| where to sell old baseball cards | 320 | $1.19–2.81 |
| where can i sell my baseball cards for cash | 260 | $1.25–3.40 |

These CPCs sit at about half of Google's all-industry average, which WordStream's 2025 benchmark puts at **$5.42 CPC, an 8.18% conversion rate and a $70.11 average cost per lead** ([WordStream 2025 benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/resources/google-ads-industry-benchmarks); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-costs-keep-rising-but-conversion-rates-improved-in-2025-477927)). **The caveat:** most of the volume is "where to sell" do-it-yourself intent, and heir-specific phrases ("inherited baseball cards") are too small to register. Inside one metro the paid volume is only a few hundred searches a month. So the desk needs a **national mail-in campaign** (vintage and graded only, prepaid insured labels) alongside the local one. **Nobody publishes a cost per *qualified* lead for this niche.** It remains the one number that decides everything, and $1,000 of ads answers it.

**Claim 2: you can buy from non-hobbyists at 50–60% of market. MOSTLY CONFIRMED, but sellers have an online anchor at 60–70%.**
- Card shops pay "50–60% of total market value in cash" for most collections, 40–60% on desirable singles and 20–40% on commons ([The Card Shop Finder](https://thecardshopfinder.com/faq/what-percentage-of-value-do-card-shops-pay/)).
- Dean's Cards says a local shop typically pays 25–50% ([Dean's Cards](https://www.deanscards.com/how-much-money-to-expect-when-i-sell-my-baseball-cards)).
- Online cash offers "typically run 60–70% of market value" ([All Vintage Cards](https://allvintagecards.com/inherited-baseball-cards/)). Card Conduit, a mail-in buyer, averages about 70%, though some reviews report around 50% after strict condition grading ([Card Conduit reviews](https://cardconduit.com/reviews/2DA0-3M)).
- **Chair's read:** plan on paying **about 50% of liquid value** and netting about 85% on resale after 8–10% blended fees, shipping and duds. That is a 30 to 35 point spread, not 50. Any seller who Googles will find the 60–70% online anchor, so your edge is speed, cash in hand, local trust and no shipping risk, not a lowball.

## 3. Real numbers

**Unit economics (middle case, one bought collection):**
- Liquid market value $2,000. Pay 50%: **$1,000**.
- Realize about 85% after fees (Fanatics Collect 0–12% depending on format, eBay about 13–15%, Whatnot about 8–11%), shipping, duds and price drift: **$1,700**.
- Minus about $50 in supplies and labels: **about $650 gross profit per buy** (65% on cost, turning in about 45 days).
- A consignment ($3,500 lot, 20% of net after about 8% fees): **about $600**, with no capital.
- A referral: **about $100** per photo-qualified lead a dealer accepts.

**The funnel (assumptions to prove in the first $1,000):**

| Step | Rate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Blended CPC | $2.75 | Keyword Planner range $1–5, verified above |
| Click → photo submission | 6–8% | WordStream all-industry average is 8.18%. A photo form converts lower |
| Cost per submission | about $35–45 | calculated |
| Submission → qualified (photos plus $750+ liquid value) | 20–25% | Most boxes are junk wax |
| **Cost per qualified lead (CPQL)** | **about $150–200** | **The number that decides the business. Target ≤ $150** |
| Qualified → buy | 35–40% | Board consensus. Dean's Cards reports 80%+ acceptance, but on pre-screened vintage |
| Qualified → consign | about 10% | |

**Month-12 middle case:** $2,000/mo of ads gives about 730 clicks and about 51 submissions, plus about 25 from free channels (estate partners, reviews, referrals), for about 76 submissions. About 17 qualify, which yields **7 buys** ($4,550) + **1.5 consigns** ($900) + **3 referrals** ($300) + bulk ($150) = **$5,900 gross**. Less ads ($2,000), partner finder's fees ($250) and tools ($200) leaves **about $3,450 a month**. At about 80 hours a month that is roughly $43/hour before tax.

**Capital:**
- First 90 days: **hard cap of $8,000** ($1,500 for ads, $6,500 for buys), plus about $300 one-time (LLC, loupe, top-loaders, scale).
- Middle case by month 6–12: about **$10–12k revolving** (7 buys of about $1,000 each, turning in about 45 days), funded from reinvested profit.
- Upside case: about $20–25k.
- Wait on the card scanner (Ricoh fi-8170, about $735–1,400) until you reach 6+ buys a month.

**Ramp (monthly net profit before tax, counted when inventory sells):**

| Month | Downside | Middle | Upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Ads $900, 0 buys, **−$900** | Ads $900, 2 buys (unsold yet), **−$500** | Ads $900, 4 buys, **+$300** |
| **3** | CPQL > $300, 2 buys; kill rule fires at day 90, **−$500** | Ads $1,200, 4 buys + 2 referrals, **+$800** | Ads $1,500, 7 buys + 1 consign, **+$2,500** |
| **6** | Stopped. Total loss locked at ≤ $2,500, **$0** | Ads $1,500, 5 buys + 1 consign, **+$2,000** | Ads $2,500, 10 buys + 2 consigns, **+$5,000** |
| **12** | Stopped, or referral/free-channel only, **$0–500** | Ads $2,000, 7 buys + 1.5 consigns, **+$3,500** | Ads $3,500, 14 buys + 3 consigns + one $15–20k vintage estate a quarter, part-time helper, **+$8,000–10,000** |

Year-1 middle total: about **$20k pre-tax profit**. The path past $5k a month runs through national mail-in for vintage, the estate-partner network passing 30% of leads, a part-time helper for shipping and scanning (Upwork, about $18/hr), reinvested capital and, optionally, the month-4+ show.

## 4. Who does what

**What Claude runs:**
- **The site.** Landing page and photo-upload intake ("Two offers in 24 hours, with the sold prices behind them") as a hosted Artifact with a shared database that doubles as the deal pipeline/CRM. Three variants: vintage sports, old Pokémon/WOTC, inherited collections. Plus 30–50 deep "what is my [set] worth" pages built from your own triage and sold log, not thousands of thin pages.
- **The photo appraisal flow.** A Routine checks new submissions every 1–2 hours from 8am to 8pm. It identifies era, set, key cards and condition flags, filters out junk wax, gives a value band, recommends a route (buy / consign / refer / decline) and drafts the two-offer email.
- **Comps.** Built only from sources whose terms allow it: 130point and manual sold checks, PriceCharting, Card Ladder, Fanatics Collect results and your own sold log. Never eBay restricted-API data inside the LLM, and never auto-buying.
- **Ads.** Builds the Google Search campaigns (local pickup radius plus national vintage mail-in) and launches them through AdWhispr once you connect the account and approve the budget. Does search-term mining, negatives and bid changes weekly, and rewrites losing ads.
- **Follow-ups.** Drafts every seller email and reminder in Gmail for your approval and books calls and pickups on Calendar. Builds and works the estate-sale company and move-manager list (150 within about 90 minutes of you), sending outreach at a human pace from your account.
- **Listings.** Variant-accurate titles and item specifics (parallel, serial, set code, language), a channel pick per card (Fanatics Collect for graded and $200+, eBay for mid-value raw, Whatnot or show lots for the middle, bulk to shops), and weekly repricing of stale listings.
- **Bookkeeping.** Per-deal P&L ledger, inventory age, decision journal (predicted vs. realized value), monthly 1099/tax summary, and a Monday dashboard showing CPQL, close rate, realized margin, share of leads from non-paid sources and the kill-rule status.
- **Fraud checklist.** Before every buy: cert lookup steps, a photo-reuse check and a vintage alteration and measurement checklist.

**Your exact tasks (about 15–20 hrs/week):**
- Form the LLC. Open Google Ads, Fanatics Collect, eBay, Whatnot, a business bank account and a payment method. Connect Google Ads to AdWhispr.
- Approve every ad budget and every offer.
- Take seller calls (Claude writes the scripts) and make pickups within about 90 minutes of home. Pay only after the cards are in hand.
- Inspect and authenticate: check every slab's cert, loupe the vintage, and get a second dealer opinion on anything over $1,000.
- Photograph or scan, pack and ship sales. Receive mail-ins.
- Sign partner and dealer agreements and vet overflow dealers with a short call.
- Protect your existing PPC client hours. The desk gets the hours you have left, not the ones your clients pay for.

## 5. The next 48 hours

Claude can start on items 2–4 and 7–8 right now.

## 6. Risk and kill rule

**Risk:** most sellers hold junk wax or kids' modern Pokémon, which can push cost per qualified lead past the math. Buying one fake or altered card can wipe out a month.

**Kill rule:** after $1,000 of ad spend, if cost per qualified lead (photos plus $750+ liquid value) is above **$150**, pause paid ads and run on partners only. If it is above $300, stop. At **day 90 (Dec 31, 2026)**, stop and sell off remaining inventory within 30 days unless you have **8+ buys netting at least $250 each and realized margin of at least 25% of market**. Maximum designed loss is about $2,500.

## 7. Runners-up

1. **Show Runner, business-1 (11 pts, 0 kills, on 7 of 9 ballots).** Promote a monthly local card show where vendors prepay for tables, so customers fund the event and you hold no card-price risk. Its middle case is about $4–6k a month from two shows. It lost because the venue contract, insurance and show-day labor are closer to a one-way door. It depends on finding an open date in an uncrowded metro (Austin had 8 shows in one month), and the board wanted the cheaper two-way test first. It is kept as the month-4+ add-on, gated on the desk passing day 90, with the refundable pre-sale (25+ paid tables by day 15 or refund everyone).
2. **Attic Appraisal Engine, growth-1 (8 pts, one first-place vote).** The same profit pool, but led by programmatic SEO and an estate-liquidator loop. As a standalone it lost because thousands of templated pages risk Google's scaled-content penalties, AI Overviews now answer "what are my cards worth" on the results page, and SEO takes 6–12 months. Its best parts (the liquidator finder's-fee loop, the shareable report and a few deep data pages) are folded into the move.

## 8. Notable dissent and the killed ideas

**Dissent (one line each):**
- **Business seat** voted leverage-1 first: make consignment the default above $3k so deal size isn't capped by bankroll. *Adopted.*
- **Leverage seat** voted first-principles-3 first: sell qualified leads to dealers so revenue scales with ad budget, not your hands. *Adopted as the overflow route, not the core.*
- **Marketing seat** voted business-mind-1 first: lead with the transparent "show the receipts" two-offer desk. *Adopted as the offer.*
- **First-principles seat:** don't hand over itemized comps before acceptance, or sellers will shop them to mail-in buyers at about 70%. *Adopted: value bands up front, itemized after acceptance.*
- **Demand seat:** sellers' real price anchor is online mail-in buyers at 60–70%, not local dealers. *Confirmed by the spot-check.*
- **Mindset seat:** the real danger is starving your working PPC income for the hobby's dopamine. Run a zero-capital PPC service (arbitrage-1 or mindset-1) alongside it. *Optional, and only if it doesn't take hours from the 90-day test.*
- **Growth seat:** if non-paid sources aren't above 30% of leads by month 6, you have built an ad arbitrage, not a business. *Added to the dashboard.*
- **Business-mind seat:** AI is not the moat. Reviews, the partner network, your own triage and sold data, and (later) an owned show date are.

**Killed ideas:**
- **marketing-2, personalized graded-card gift (8 kills):** about $55 gross margin against about $38 Meta cost per purchase, no repeat buyers, and Pokémon trademark rules strip the hook from the ads.
- **business-mind-3, collection legacy plan for older collectors (6 kills):** about $1.5–2k a month by its own math, no evidence anyone pays for it, and the payoff is years out.
- **growth-3, Naruto Card Game fan hub (5 kills):** revenue starts after month 10, it depends on an unlaunched Bandai game, and its ad and affiliate income is thin.
- **One kill each:** leverage-2 (accessory brand: commodity, tariffs, misses Q4), growth-2 (live appraisal show: your camera hours are the product), arbitrage-3 (shop aged-inventory desk: repricing cuts the client's own margin).
