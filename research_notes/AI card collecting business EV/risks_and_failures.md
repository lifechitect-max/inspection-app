# Risks, Failure Modes and Negative-EV Traps in Trading Card Businesses (as of 2026-10-01)

> Method note for the report writer: research was done 2026-10-01 via web search. Nearly every primary hobby/news domain I tried to open in full (si.com, sportico.com, cardlines.com, psacard.com, nerdbeak.com, thecardshopfinder.com) was blocked by the network egress proxy, so most findings below rest on **search-result summaries of those pages, not full-text reads**. Treat specific numbers as "reported by" the cited source and check them before quoting them as hard fact. Where a source looks low quality (SEO or AI-generated blogs), I say so.

## 1. Market risk: crashes, froth, overprinting, sealed declines, modern dilution

### Takeaway
The hobby has crashed hard twice in living memory. The first was the 1990s junk-wax overprinting era (historical). The second was the 2022 post-pandemic correction, when modern cards fell roughly 50-73% from their early-2021 peaks while vintage held up. In 2025-2026 the main structural risk is supply. Pokemon print runs have risen sharply, so in-print sealed product trades at or below MSRP. A business that holds modern inventory is fighting dilution, and its returns depend on timing, not on holding.

### Cited Findings
- **2022 correction (historical):** the 2020-21 surge showed "300-1000% price increases followed by 50-70% corrections in 2022" — [PreGradeCards bubble analysis](https://pregradecards.com/blog/sports-card-market-bubble-analysis) (a secondary blog, so treat the range as approximate)
- Single-card examples: Kobe Bryant 1996 Topps PSA 10 down 72% and Michael Jordan 1986 Fleer PSA 9 down 73% from February 2021. A 2003-04 LeBron James rookie fell from nearly $300,000 to below $50,000 — [SI Collectibles, "5 Superstar Rookie Cards That Have Lost the Most Value Since 2020"](https://www.si.com/collectibles/news/5-superstar-rookie-cards-that-have-lost-the-most-value-since-2020); see also [fusion94, "When Cards Outpaced Stocks 2016-2024"](https://fusion94.org/2025/07/21/when-cards-outpaced-stocks-comparing-market-bubbles-from-2016-2024)
- During the 2022 drawdown, modern cards fell sharply while "the market for vintage cards held strong". Player indexes cited: Mike Trout -40.87%, Kobe Bryant -36.26%, Tom Brady -22.2% over the period measured — [Sports Collectors Digest, card market values / PWCC & Card Ladder](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/sports-card-market-values-vintage-cards-modern-pwcc-card-ladder)
- Niche-category collapse example: the Formula 1 card market "has collapsed" after its boom — [CLLCT](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/sports-cards/formula-1-sports-card-market-has-collapsed-but-it-s-not-dead-yet)
- **Pokemon print runs (2025-2026):** The Pokemon Company raised production for Brilliant Stars, Lost Origin, Crown Zenith, Pokemon 151, Prismatic Evolutions and later sets to quantities that "dwarf earlier sets", which eroded the scarcity premium — [Misprint, "Why Pokemon Card Print Runs Are Bigger Than Ever in 2026"](https://www.misprint.com/posts/why-pokemon-print-runs-bigger-2026); [Misprint, "Why Are Pokemon Card Prices Dropping? (2026)"](https://www.misprint.com/posts/why-are-pokemon-card-prices-dropping)
- **Sealed declines:** Phantasmal Flames booster boxes fell from $305 to $275 in ten days, and ETBs fell from $120 to $90 (-25%). Sealed product averaged a 15-25% decline since March 2025. In-print sets (Destined Rivals, Surging Sparks, Journey Together) trade "at or slightly below MSRP". Out-of-print Evolving Skies boxes rose from about $220 to $260-310 in a year — [Misprint sealed price trends 2026](https://www.misprint.com/posts/pokemon-sealed-product-price-trends-2026)
- TCGplayer publishes weekly "biggest price drops" columns for Pokemon through 2026, e.g. [07/22/2026](https://www.tcgplayer.com/content/article/The-Biggest-Price-Drops-in-Pok%C3%A9mon-this-Week-07-22-2026/bd218cd0-ee67-47eb-8aa1-d0d77db6429d/) and [02/18/2026](https://seller.tcgplayer.com/blog/price-trends-pokémon-cards-dropping-in-price-02-18-2026)
- "Crash signs" commentary for 2026 Pokemon — [PokemonPriceTracker, "Pokemon Card Market Crash 2026: Signs & How to Prepare"](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/blog/posts/pokemon-card-market-crash-2026-signs-how-to-prepare) (opinion piece)
- **September 2026 sports card softness (LOW RELIABILITY):** one site reports month-over-month drops in September 2026 of about 12% for raw baseball cards and about 10% for PSA 10s, plus 12-20% for some hockey rookies and a post-World-Cup cooldown in soccer — [cardcenteringtool blog](https://blog.cardcenteringtool.com/baseball-card-prices-dropping-september-2026-market-insights-tips/). **Caution:** this site published about 10 near-duplicate "prices dropping September 2026" posts and looks like AI or SEO content, so do not rely on its numbers.

### Inferences
- Modern product is printed to meet demand. Manufacturers (Fanatics/Topps, The Pokemon Company) gain from selling more units, and that works directly against scarcity-based appreciation. Buying modern sealed product to hold is likely negative-EV after fees, storage and capital cost, unless the set goes out of print and demand persists (the Evolving Skies pattern), which is hard to predict.
- Vintage and blue-chip cards held value better in 2022. A business that depends on the newest release cycle carries the most drawdown risk.
- Booms tend to end with 50-70% modern drawdowns. Inventory bought near a peak can take years to recover, or may never recover.

### Gaps
- I could not get primary Card Ladder index data for 2025-2026, such as the CL50 index and year-to-date change. Card Ladder pages were not fetched.
- No reliable 2026 data on the total sports card market size or sales volume trend.
- The junk-wax (1987-1994) overproduction story is well known, but I did not fetch a primary source with print-run figures in this session.

## 2. Counterfeits and fraud

### Takeaway
Fraud is large and growing fastest in Pokemon. PSA reports intercepting more than $200M in counterfeit and altered items. Counterfeit Pokemon cards rose 125% year over year and altered Pokemon cards rose 407%. Fake slabs now clone real cert numbers, so a cert lookup alone is no longer enough. Market manipulation has also reached the biggest players: PWCC was banned from eBay in 2021 over shill bidding.

### Cited Findings
- PSA's fraud report: more than **$200 million** in counterfeit and altered collectibles were intercepted before reaching slabs — [SI Collectibles, "$200M in Fake Cards"](https://www.si.com/collectibles/psa-report-reveals-200m-fake-cards)
- PSA's 2025 fraud report: counterfeit Pokemon cards **+125% YoY** and altered Pokemon cards **+407%** — [SI Collectibles](https://www.si.com/collectibles/psa-report-reveals-200m-fake-cards) (search-snippet summary; full report not opened)
- Fraud types PSA reports: rebacked vintage cards, trimmed edges, altered surfaces, fake autographs and counterfeit slabs — [SI Collectibles](https://www.si.com/collectibles/psa-report-reveals-200m-fake-cards); see also [AOL, "Fake cards, real money"](https://www.aol.com/articles/fake-cards-real-money-rising-190827234.html)
- "Cert cloning": a scammer copies a real cert number from completed eBay listing photos and prints a fake label for a fake card of the same player and set. PSA warns that criminals use genuine cert numbers on counterfeit inserts — [Slabfy fake slab checklist](https://slabfy.com/blog/how-to-spot-fake-graded-slabs); [PokeSats](https://pokesats.com/newsroom/how-to-spot-a-fake-psa-slab/)
- One report describes a forged-PSA-label scheme worth about **$2M** — [cardsmania](https://cardsmania.fun/how-forged-psa-labels-fueled-2m-trading/) (low-authority site; verify before citing)
- **PWCC shill bidding (historical, 2021):** eBay banned PWCC, then one of its largest card sellers with more than **$200M** in eBay sales in the prior year, over "shill bidding" by associated individuals. PWCC denied knowledge — [Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/pwcc-marketplace-shill-bidding-accusation-ebay-restrictions-sports-cards); [Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/news/ebay-pulls-biggest-trading-card-seller-off-market-shill-bidding-pwcc)
- PWCC was "already under investigation by the FBI for card trimming". Fanatics bought PWCC in May 2023, after PWCC laid off about three dozen staff, and renamed it Fanatics Collect. Alt later sued PWCC/Fanatics Collect, alleging a "widespread, years-long" shill bidding scheme — [Sports Collectors Digest, Alt lawsuit](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/trading-card-company-alt-files-lawsuit-against-pwcc); [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/alt-lawsuit-against-pwcc-shill-bidding/); [SCD Fanatics / Huigens part ways](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/fanatics-pwcc-founder-brent-huigens-part-ways/)
- **Product-integrity litigation (2026):** *Sanchez v. Topps* was filed January 29, 2026. It alleges distribution manipulation and fraud over product contents — [search summary citing thecardshopfinder / Cardlines](https://thecardshopfinder.com/blog/breaks-repacks-legal-scrutiny/). Separately, allegations surfaced about Topps Chrome Sapphire "hit parades" and preferential treatment of certain breakers (New Backyard Breaks) — [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/topps-chrome-sapphire-hit-parade-reignites-new-backyard-breaks-preferential-treatment-allegations/)

### Inferences
- Fraud costs a small operator in three ways: (a) buying a fake or altered card, (b) chargebacks or "item not as described" claims, which can be abused by buyers who swap a real card for a fake on return, and (c) a reputational hit if they unknowingly sell a fake. Pokemon has the highest current fraud growth.
- If insiders at big auction houses inflated comps through shill bidding, public "sold" prices can overstate true liquidity. That distorts any EV model built on comps.

### Gaps
- No hard 2025-2026 data found on chargeback or return-swap fraud rates on eBay or Whatnot. Searches returned fee pages only.
- No reliable source found on AI-generated fakes specifically, such as AI-made card images or fake listing photos, beyond general mentions. This is a gap.
- I did not confirm the current status of the Alt v. PWCC/Fanatics Collect lawsuit or the FBI trimming probe.

## 3. Legal and regulatory: gambling suits, taxes, tariffs

### Takeaway
Live breaks and repacks are now under real legal attack. By March 2026, attorney Paul Lesko had filed 15 arbitration demands for 30 clients against Whatnot, alleging an illegal lottery under California Penal Code 319/319.3 and RICO, and he expects about 100 cases. A qui tam suit against Whatnot and Fanatics Live has been unsealed, and Topps faces a new gambling-related class action. Breaking is therefore the highest-legal-risk business model. On taxes, federal 1099-K reporting reverted to more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions (OBBBA, July 2025), but income is still taxable whether or not a form is issued. Tariffs have pushed up supply costs, especially accessories.

### Cited Findings
- **Whatnot arbitrations:** Paul Lesko filed 15 arbitration demands for 30 clients in March 2026, with more expected. They allege randomized box breaks and repack breaks violate RICO, California's illegal-lottery ban, and California's specific ban on sports trading card "grab bag" lotteries (Penal Code 319.3). The filings call Whatnot an "unregulated online casino" and seek restitution, warnings, spending limits, punitive damages and a declaration that the activity is unlawful — [Baseball America](https://www.baseballamerica.com/stories/lawsuit-accuses-breaking-platform-whatnot-of-being-unregulated-online-casino-for-sports-cards-report/); [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/new-legal-action-suggests-whatnot-is-unlawful-gambling-scheme/); [Nerdbeak, March 2026](https://www.nerdbeak.com/news/whatnot-gambling-lawsuits-illegal-casino-march-2026)
- Counsel expects about **100 total cases**. Because these are individual arbitrations, not a class action, each claimant files separately — [SettlementInsight](https://settlementinsight.com/whatnot-lawsuit) (aggregator)
- Whatnot's valuation is reported as $11.5B by Nerdbeak and about $8B in a Substack headline. **These conflict**, possibly because they refer to different funding rounds — [Nerdbeak](https://www.nerdbeak.com/news/whatnot-gambling-lawsuits-illegal-casino-march-2026); [Substack](https://substack.com/home/post/p-191176334)
- A **qui tam suit against Whatnot and Fanatics Live has been unsealed**. Law firms are issuing advisories warning breakers and repack sellers about gambling and lottery statutes. The core legal theory is California PC 319's three elements: payment (the spot), chance (the team randomizer) and prize (the cards) — [thecardshopfinder, "breaks & repacks legal scrutiny"](https://thecardshopfinder.com/blog/breaks-repacks-legal-scrutiny/) (search summary only; page blocked)
- Topps "has been hit with another gambling-related class action, roughly two decades after the first one". The first refers to the late-1990s/2000s insert-card gambling RICO suits, which ultimately failed, as historical context — [same search summary](https://thecardshopfinder.com/blog/breaks-repacks-legal-scrutiny/)
- **1099-K:** OBBBA (P.L. 119-21) §70432, signed July 4, 2025, restored the federal threshold to **more than $20,000 AND more than 200 transactions**, retroactive to years after 2021. That covers tax years 2025 and 2026 — [CPA Practice Advisor, Feb 2026](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/02/18/2026-tax-filing-and-form-1099-k-know-the-new-rules-this-tax-season/178331/); [Beancount explainer](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/15/1099-k-threshold-whiplash-2026-gig-workers-online-sellers-reconcile-marketplace-forms-20000-200-transactions-obbba-guide). Some platforms, such as eBay, may still issue 1099-Ks below the threshold, and some states keep lower state thresholds — [Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/tax/library/irs-forms/1099-k-threshold/)
- **Tariffs:** tariffs on Chinese imports raised prices for sleeves, tins and international product. Parts of some Pokemon products, such as ETB components, are made in China. One retailer reported a 25% supplier price increase on accessories — [SNKRDUNK magazine](https://snkrdunk.com/en/magazine/?p=426279); [CLLCT, "Graders, card companies brace for impact of tariffs"](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/memorabilia/graders-card-companies-brace-for-impact-of-tariffs); [The Hockey News, "Taxed out"](https://thehockeynews.com/news/latest-news/taxed-out-trade-wars-have-hurt-hockey-card-collectors)

### Inferences
- A business built on randomized breaks or mystery repacks faces tail risk. Even if the platforms win, rules on randomizers, age verification and spending limits could arrive by platform policy change faster than by court ruling. Breakers who rely on a single platform carry both legal and platform risk.
- The higher 1099-K threshold reduces paperwork but not tax liability. Collectibles held as investments face a 28% long-term capital gains rate, and dealers owe ordinary and self-employment tax. Sales tax on in-person show and shop sales stays a state-level obligation. The tax treatment is general knowledge; no source was found in this session.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the case number, court or filing date of the Whatnot/Fanatics Live qui tam suit, or the docket for the new Topps gambling class action.
- No state AG or FTC action specific to card breaks or mystery boxes was found in 2025-2026. Searches surfaced none, but that does not prove there are none.
- I did not verify the specific 2026 tariff rates applying to trading cards or the HTS classification.

## 4. Platform risk: fees, bans, grading bottlenecks, licensing

### Takeaway
Operators depend on a few chokepoints: eBay, Whatnot, PSA and Fanatics/Topps. In 2026 all four shifted against small operators. PSA raised prices in February and paused all Value tiers on June 2, 2026, with a backlog reported at about 14M cards, so the cheapest grading is now about $79.99 per card. Whatnot restructured commissions on September 21, 2026. eBay charges about 13% plus. Fanatics now controls most major league licenses, and Panini's antitrust case will not reach trial before 2027-28.

### Cited Findings
- **PSA February 10, 2026 increase:** Value Bulk $21.99 to $24.99; Value $27.99 to $32.99; Value Plus $44.99 to $49.99; Value Max $59.99 to $64.99; Regular $74.99 to $79.99. Turnaround also grew by 5 days for Value Plus, Value Max and Regular. PSA said this was meant to slow submissions — [DK Network, Feb 10, 2026](https://dknetwork.draftkings.com/2026/02/10/psa-grading-price-increase-2026/); [SI Collectibles](https://www.si.com/collectibles/psa-increasing-card-grading-prices-and-turnaround-times)
- **PSA paused all four Value tiers on June 2, 2026.** A roughly 20% spike in submissions followed PSA's announced $200M infrastructure investment, and the backlog then hit a reported **14 million cards**, an all-time high. The tiers are expected to reopen when the backlog falls to about 5M. PSA launched a public Backlog Tracker — [PreGradeCards](https://pregradecards.com/blog/psa-pauses-value-tiers-june-2026); [thecardshopfinder](https://thecardshopfinder.com/blog/psa-backlog-14-million/); [PSA Backlog Tracker](https://www.psacard.com/info/backlog-tracker). A later SI headline refers to the backlog "falling to 12 million" — [SI](https://www.si.com/collectibles/psa-backlog-falls-12-million-means-card-grading-2026)
- Grading now effectively starts at **$79.99 per card** while the Value tiers are paused — [cardgrade.io](https://cardgrade.io/psa-grading) / search summary
- **Whatnot fees:** a new tiered commission took effect **September 21, 2026**. For Sports and TCG it runs from 8.00% (Standard, $0-14,999) down to 6.50% (Tier 6, $500K+), plus payment processing — [Voolist](https://www.voolist.com/blog/whatnot-fees-2026); [Whatnot help center](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/4847069165965-Whatnot-seller-fees)
- **eBay:** most collectibles categories charge a 12.7% final value fee on the first $2,500 and 2.35% above that, plus a per-order fee. The US currency conversion charge rises to 3.25% from October 14, 2026 — [eBay selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822); [eBay store fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/store-selling-fees-managed-payments-sellers?id=4809)
- **Fanatics/Panini:** Panini sued in 2023 over Fanatics' exclusive licenses. Fanatics now holds MLB, plus NFL and NBA deals starting 2026. The case largely survived dismissal and is in discovery through end-2026, with trial expected in 2027-28. A separate consumer antitrust class action against Fanatics was dismissed in 2026 for lack of standing — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/fanatics-panini-licenses-antitrust-lawsuit/); [Law360](https://www.law360.com/employment-authority/other/articles/2308447/panini-trading-card-antitrust-suit-largely-beats-dismissal); [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/law/analysis/2026/fanatics-trading-cards-antitrust-lawsuit-dismissal-1234888676/); [Athlon](https://athlonsports.com/collectibles/fanatics-monopoly-lawsuit-dismissed-2026-antitrust-explainer)
- Platform bans can be sudden even for the biggest sellers, as with PWCC's eBay ban (2021) — [SCD](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/pwcc-marketplace-shill-bidding-accusation-ebay-restrictions-sports-cards)

### Inferences
- The grade-and-flip model is badly squeezed. At $79.99 or more per card plus shipping and insurance, a modern card needs a PSA 10 premium of more than $100 just to break even, before counting gem-rate risk (most submissions are not 10s) and months of locked capital.
- Fanatics' vertical integration (Topps manufacturing, Fanatics Live breaking, Fanatics Collect marketplace and vault) means a small operator competes against the licensor itself.

### Gaps
- Current PSA turnaround times by tier as of October 2026 are unknown, because PSA pages were blocked.
- No data found on Whatnot account bans or suspension rates, or on breaker deplatforming.

## 5. Capital and inventory risk

### Takeaway
The main capital traps are cash stuck in grading queues for months, cash stuck in in-print sealed product that has fallen 15-25%, and the fees, shipping and grading costs that sellers routinely underestimate. One source says these hidden costs make margins up to 39% worse than estimated.

### Cited Findings
- Businesses "often miscalculate sports card margins by up to 39%" because they overlook grading, storage and channel fees — [search summary, card-flipping guide](https://thecardshopfinder.com/guides/sports-cards/card-flipping-guide/) (low-authority; directional only)
- Marketplace fees take roughly 10-13% of every sale, so flips need a wide buy/sell spread — [Giant Sports Cards blog](https://giantsportscards.com/blogs/blog/flipping-baseball-cards-for-profit-strategies-for-maximizing-roi); [eBay fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)
- The PSA backlog reached 14M cards, the Value tiers are paused and turnaround times have increased (see Section 4). Capital sent to grading is illiquid for months — [PreGradeCards](https://pregradecards.com/blog/psa-pauses-value-tiers-june-2026)
- In-print sealed Pokemon trades at or below MSRP, so buying at retail and selling on the secondary market leaves little or no margin until the set goes out of print — [Misprint](https://www.misprint.com/posts/pokemon-sealed-product-price-trends-2026)
- **Fractional/startup failures (historical, 2023-2024):** Dibbs, backed by Amazon, shut its marketplace in March 2023. Collectable announced liquidation in May 2023, was sold for $1.6M in June 2023, and sunset its marketplace with a December 13, 2024 withdrawal deadline. Investors complained items were being "held hostage". Otis also failed or pivoted. Rally still operates, but its parent reported a $10.3M accumulated deficit in FY2025 with a going-concern note from its auditor — [SI, "Fractional Investing in Sports Cards Has Been A Failure"](https://www.si.com/collectibles/news/fractional-investing-in-sports-cards-has-been-a-failure); [CLLCT](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/memorabilia/investors-in-collectable-concerned-they-are-holding-our-items-hostage); [Angel Investors Network, Rally review 2026](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/alternative-investments/rally-rd-review-2026); [Alts.co](https://alts.co/the-state-of-fractional-investing/)
- PWCC laid off about three dozen staff before Fanatics bought it in 2023 — [SCD](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/pwcc-marketplace-allegations-ban-response-reaction-vault)

### Inferences
- Inventory risk compounds. A card bought at peak, sent to grading for 3-6 months, returned as a PSA 9 instead of a 10, and then sold into a down market can easily lose 30-60% of the capital involved.
- The fractional and collectibles fintech wave of 2020-2023 mostly failed within 2-3 years once trading volume fell. Software and marketplace startups in this space face the same boom-bust cycle as card prices.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found on shipping damage or loss rates (eBay, USPS, PSA return shipping) for cards.
- StarStock's shutdown or pivot was not confirmed in search results.
- No data found on typical PSA gem rates for modern submissions.

## 6. Competition, failure stories, and what separates winners

### Takeaway
Flipping, breaking and content are crowded. The licensor (Fanatics) and the large platforms (Whatnot, eBay) capture structural margin. Shop closures in 2025-2026 cite online competition, falling sales and short leases. The thin evidence on winners points to buying below market, high turnover over big margins, cost discipline, and holding vintage or blue-chip cards rather than modern speculation. Direct, high-quality first-person data on quitters was not retrievable in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Shop closures: Sports Collectors Daily keeps a running "sports card shop closing" tag, with recent entries such as a KY shop owner quitting after 29 years and a long-running PA shop "selling out" — [SCD tag](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/tag/sports-card-shop-closing/); [KY shop](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/ky-shop-owner-calling-it-quits-after-29-years/); [PA shop](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/long-running-pa-shop-selling-out/)
- Reasons cited across closures: online competition, falling sales, leases under 5 years with no renewal guarantee, and retirement or family. UK chain Cardzone closed 9 shops in 2026 (non-US, for context) — [Spokane Journal](https://www.spokanejournal.com/local-news/south-hill-retailer-to-close-its-doors); [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/full-list-cardzone-shops-close-uk-2026-5HjdfCM_2/); [KDKA/Audacy](https://www.audacy.com/kdkaradio/news/local/card-shop-closing)
- Allegations that manufacturers or big breakers receive preferential hit distribution (Topps Chrome Sapphire / New Backyard Breaks) suggest small breakers may not compete on equal product quality — [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/topps-chrome-sapphire-hit-parade-reignites-new-backyard-breaks-preferential-treatment-allegations/)
- Winner traits from practitioner guides: "every flip starts with buying low". Turnover beats margin, because a card with a big margin but no sales is worth less than a small-margin card that sells constantly. Some dealers aim for about 50% gross margins — [Giant Sports Cards](https://giantsportscards.com/blogs/blog/flipping-baseball-cards-for-profit-strategies-for-maximizing-roi); [CardPulse](https://www.cardpulse.club/blog/make-money-trading-sports-cards); [Collectors Universe forum](https://forums.collectors.com/discussion/comment/11269463) (practitioner opinion, not data)
- Vintage held value during the 2022 drawdown while modern fell, which favors operators with vintage or blue-chip expertise — [SCD](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/sports-card-market-values-vintage-cards-modern-pwcc-card-ladder)

### Inferences
- The structural winners are the toll collectors: the manufacturer, the grader, the marketplace and the breaking platform. Most small operators pay those tolls (about 8-13% marketplace fees, $25-80+ per card grading, product bought at or near distributor price) and then compete on thin spreads against better-capitalized dealers and insiders.
- Operators who survive tend to have (a) sourcing edges such as estate buys, show buying and bulk collections bought at deep discounts, (b) category expertise in vintage authentication or grading prediction, (c) high turnover with low inventory days, and (d) several sales channels. These are the patterns implied by the sources above, not proven by data.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/sportscards, r/PokemonTCG, r/flipping) first-person "why I quit" threads were not retrieved in this session. No quantified survey of breaker or flipper profitability was found.
- No data found on bot sniping or retail-drop bots for Pokemon in 2026, though it is widely discussed anecdotally.
- No published survival-rate statistic for card shops or breakers was found.
