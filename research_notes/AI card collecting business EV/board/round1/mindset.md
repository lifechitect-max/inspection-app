# Mindset Expert: Round 1 Proposals

**Lens:** What is really stopping us from acting today, and which bets are sized so that no single loss is fatal?
**Date:** 2026-10-01

## My read before the ideas (bias check on the board and the user)

1. **The hobby is a trap for the mind before it is a trap for capital.** Cards are built to trigger dopamine: chase cards, pack rips, "it's going up." A newcomer who says "I'm starting a card business" usually buys inventory first and asks questions later. Our own research shows the result. Modern sealed is down 20-50% from its peak. Flip margins have collapsed from 100-200% to 10-20% or negative ([ICv2, flipper meltdown](https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/63449/pokmon-tcg-flippers-meltdown-discord-wave-schadenfreude-washes-over-some-fans); [PokemonPriceTracker](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/blog/posts/pokemon-card-market-crash-2026-what-collectors-must-know)). A part-time flipper makes about $7-14/hour (business_models note, section 7). Grading arbitrage is shut while PSA Value tiers are paused. Random breaks face gambling claims.
2. **Shiny-object risk.** The user already runs a working Amazon PPC freelance business. The most expensive mistake would be to starve that engine to chase a hobby that is in a late-boom phase. Every move below either **builds on the user's current skill** (ads, marketplaces) or is **a capped bet with a written kill rule**.
3. **Survivor bias.** Whatnot's "66% of sellers earn $10k+/mo" comes from a survey of active sellers. People who quit don't post. Plan around the median outcome, not the highlight reel.
4. **What the user controls:** offers, outreach volume, buying discipline, ad creative. **What the user doesn't control:** Pokemon print runs, PSA backlog, Whatnot lawsuits, card prices. So I favor moves whose profit comes from **transaction volume and services**. Volume is at a record (eBay focus categories +26%, Whatnot $8B+ in H1 2026), and those moves don't depend on prices going up.

My three moves form a **commitment ladder**. Each step is a two-way door, and each step teaches you something that makes the next one safer.

## mindset-1: Home-Field Niche (Amazon PPC for the hobby's picks-and-shovels brands)

- **One-liner:** Don't become a card dealer. Become *the* Amazon PPC specialist for trading-card accessory and supply brands (sleeves, toploaders, binders, magnetic holders, display cases, storage boxes, playmats, deck boxes).
- **The hack:** The structural winners in this hobby are the toll collectors. Accessories earn 65-70% gross margin, versus about 20% on cards at a shop. The accessory category is having its best growth since 2019. Those brands fight on Amazon, which is the user's home field. Niching down turns a commodity "Amazon PPC freelancer" into "the hobby's PPC person." The niche positioning justifies a higher retainer and gives you referral loops inside a small industry. It needs **zero inventory and zero capital**, and as a side benefit it gives you a front-row view of category data, so you'll know if launching your own accessory product (Round 2 option) is worth it.
- **Why now:**
  - Online sales of TCG games and accessories were about $1.15B in 2025, and 2026 revenue is expected to grow 50%+ ([Grips Intelligence](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/industries/arts-entertainment/trading-card-games-and-accessories)).
  - 2025 was accessories' strongest growth year since 2019 ([Cardcosmos 2025 review](https://cardcosmos.de/en/blogs/news/sammelkartentrends-2025-ruckblick-analyse)).
  - Top Amazon listings for toploaders and sleeves sell 10k-40k units a month at $15-25 ([AsInsight sleeves](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/card-sleeves); [AsInsight top loaders](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/sports-card-top-loaders)).
  - Vault X is estimated at $1.7-3.1M a month in revenue with "very active advertising" ([BrandSearch/Spectre](https://brandsearch.co/brands/vaultx.com), a third-party estimate).
  - Tariffs on China-made supplies squeeze margins ([CLLCT](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/sports-cards/i-m-getting-screwed-card-supply-companies-prepare-for-tariffs-to-hit)). Squeezed brands care more about ad efficiency (ACoS).
  - Q4 (holiday plus the Pokemon 30th anniversary waves on Oct 30 and Nov 6) is the peak ad season. The window to pitch is **now**.
- **How Claude runs it:**
  - **Prospect list.** Claude pulls the ranked brand list from Amazon search pages and AsInsight-style reports for about 30 accessory keywords. It finds the brand owners' websites and contact pages, and scores each brand on visible ad gaps: no Sponsored Brands video, weak titles, missing A+ content, low review velocity.
  - **Audits.** For each target, Claude writes a 1-page "outside-in" audit plus a Loom script. It finds keyword gaps using AdWhispr's Meta/Google competitor-ad research and Amazon SERP checks.
  - **Outreach.** Claude drafts personalized emails as Gmail drafts, never bulk spam: 10-15 per day, one-to-one, CAN-SPAM compliant. It also writes Upwork proposals in the user's voice for any collectibles or hobby PPC job.
  - **Client work.** Once clients sign, Claude runs weekly bulk-sheet analysis (search term mining, negatives, bid changes) and writes client reports on a Routine. The user approves the changes.
- **User's tasks:**
  - Sales calls (2-4 per week).
  - Final approval on bid changes.
  - Logging into client Seller Central and Ads consoles. Claude can't create accounts.
  - Reposition the Upwork profile headline and portfolio (about 1 hour).
  - About 3-6 hours a week per client after onboarding.
- **First 30 days:**
  - **Day 1-2.** Claude builds a 60-brand prospect sheet and 10 audits. The user rewrites the Upwork headline to "Amazon PPC for trading card and collectibles brands."
  - **Day 3-10.** Send 10 personalized emails/DMs per weekday (about 70 total). Also send proposals on any hobby or collectibles jobs.
  - **Day 11-20.** Offer a 30-day "Q4 sprint" at a founding rate ($1,250/mo, or $750 + 5% of ad spend) to the first 2 clients in exchange for a case study.
  - **Day 21-30.** Deliver the first weekly report. Ask for referrals (accessory founders know each other through distributors and conventions).
- **Path to the goal (math):**
  - Retainers in 2026 cluster at $1,500-5,000/mo, or 10-20% of ad spend ([SalesDuo](https://salesduo.com/blog/amazon-ppc-management-cost-agency-fees/); [ecomBrainly](https://ecombrainly.com/amazon-advertising-agencies/)).
  - **Price.** Assume a niche-small-brand rate of $1,500-2,000/mo.
  - **Volume.** A 70-message burst a month at about 3% to a call and a 30% close rate gives roughly 0.6 clients a month. With referrals and Upwork, assume **1 new client every 6-8 weeks**, with 15% monthly churn.
  - **Margin.** About 90%. Costs are tools only.
  - **Honest middle at month 12:** 4 clients × $1,750 = **$7,000/mo profit** on about 15-20 hours a week.
  - **Downside:** 1-2 clients, so $1,750-3,500/mo, which is still positive and needs no capital at risk.
  - **Upside:** 6-8 clients plus a performance fee, or using the data to launch your own accessory SKU.
- **Biggest risk:** Many top sellers are China-based brands (e.g., Arjiekwei) that are hard to reach or run ads in-house. The reachable pool of US/EU brands may be about 50-150. Mitigate by widening to "hobby e-commerce": grading-supply, display-case and Shopify card stores running Google/Meta.
- **Kill rule:** If 150 personalized touches by Nov 15, 2026 produce fewer than 4 sales calls, stop the niche positioning and fold the learnings back into general PPC. Zero capital lost.
- **Confidence: 8/10.** Highest +EV and lowest variance. The user already has the skill, so this is a positioning move, not a new business.

## mindset-2: The Collection Desk (ads-driven collection buying with a Claude pricing desk, capped bankroll)

- **One-liner:** Use the user's paid-ads skill to source cards *below market* by buying whole collections from the public. Run ad-generated "sell your old cards, offer in 24 hours" leads through a Claude photo-triage desk, then liquidate through the cheapest channels.
- **The hack:** Every source in our research agrees: the money is in **buying**, not selling. Collections at 50-60% of market beat any channel optimization, and dealers commonly pay 60-80% of market ([thecardshopfinder FAQ](https://thecardshopfinder.com/faq/what-percentage-of-value-do-card-shops-pay/)). Most card dealers source from shows and word of mouth. Almost none run disciplined Meta/Google lead funnels with offer-in-24-hours speed. The user does. Claude removes the pricing bottleneck: it identifies cards from seller photos, flags keys (WOTC-era Pokemon 1999-2003, pre-1980 sports, 2000s+ rookies and graded slabs), screens out junk wax, and drafts the offer. This is the only "real card business" I'd let a newcomer touch, because the **edge sits in sourcing, which the user controls**, not in price direction.
- **Why now:**
  - 19% of US adults bought Pokemon cards in 6 months ([Retail Brew/Circana](https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2025/06/06/collectible-trading-cards-and-adults-drive-toy-sales-in-2025-circana)). Many 30-45-year-olds have childhood binders sitting in closets, and record headlines (the $16.49M Pikachu) push them to "check what it's worth."
  - Volume and liquidity are at records, so exits are fast. Fanatics Collect charges 6%, or 0% with FanCash.
  - Facebook "Hobbies & Leisure" clicks are cheap: median CPC $0.56 ([AuctionWriter](https://auctionwriter.com/10-proven-facebook-ad-strategies-to-drive-off%E2%80%91platform-bidders-to-your-estate-auction/), a weak source).
  - Cards I'd target (vintage, WOTC) held value in the 2022 drawdown, while modern product fell.
- **How Claude runs it:**
  - **Funnel.** Claude builds the landing page and intake form (photo upload) as a hosted page. It writes 10 ad variants and launches small Meta/Google local campaigns via AdWhispr at a 30-50 mile radius, at $20-40 a day.
  - **Triage.** It sorts each lead into: junk wax / bulk (decline politely or refer to a partner shop), worth a look, or priority.
  - **Pricing.** It IDs the cards and builds a comp sheet from public sold data the user checks manually. No piping of eBay restricted-API data into the LLM, no auto-buy. It drafts a written offer at 50-65% of liquid value.
  - **Admin.** It schedules meetups via Calendar and drafts follow-ups in Gmail.
  - **Liquidation.** After purchase, it writes variant-accurate listings and picks the channel (Fanatics Collect for graded/high value, eBay for raw mid-value, bulk to local shops). It keeps the P&L ledger and the decision journal.
- **User's tasks:**
  - Meet sellers in person (safe public spots or shops), inspect, pay and transport.
  - Photograph, pack and ship.
  - Final say on every offer.
  - Create the ad, eBay and Fanatics accounts.
  - Learn the counterfeit and alteration basics (verify certs, use a loupe).
  - About 12-20 hours a week.
- **First 30 days:**
  - **Day 1-3.** Write the fear-setting page (below). Claude builds the landing page and 10 ads. Set the bankroll at **$8,000 hard cap** for 90 days, and $1,500 of that for ads.
  - **Day 4-14.** Run $25/day. Target 40 leads. Make offers on the top 25%.
  - **Day 15-30.** Close 3-5 buys and list everything within 72 hours of purchase. Track cost per lead, close rate, average buy price as % of market, and days to sell.
- **Path to the goal (math):**
  - **Ads.** $750/month gives about 35 leads at about $20 CPL (assumed). 20% are worth an offer and 50% of offers close, so **about 3-4 buys a month** at the start, rising to 6-8 as creative and referrals improve.
  - **Per buy (middle case).**
    - Collection market value $2,400, bought at 58% = **$1,400**.
    - Liquidate at about 88% of market after fees, shipping and bulk discount = $2,110.
    - Gross profit = **$710**. Minus the ad cost per acquired collection (about $190) = **about $520 net per buy**.
  - **At 6 buys a month (month 6+):** about $3,100/mo.
  - **At 10 buys a month with referrals and repeat sellers:** about $5,200/mo.
  - **Working capital:** about $8-12k turning about monthly.
  - **Honest middle at month 12: $3,000-4,500/mo.** Lumpy, with one great vintage find a quarter that can add $2-5k.
  - **Downside:** junk-heavy leads, so around breakeven, and you lose about $1,500 in ads plus some slow inventory.
- **Biggest risk:**
  - Lead quality. Most "old collections" are 1987-1994 junk wax, worth almost nothing.
  - Buying a fake or altered card. Altered Pokemon cards are up 407% YoY ([SI/PSA fraud report](https://www.si.com/collectibles/psa-report-reveals-200m-fake-cards)).
  - Ethics: pay fair, transparent offers and never lowball unsophisticated sellers (widows, kids). It's the right thing, and your reputation is the referral engine.
- **Kill rule:** At day 90 (Dec 30, 2026), stop if **net profit per buy is under $250 or there are fewer than 8 total buys**. Liquidate remaining inventory within 30 days. The maximum loss is designed at about $2,500 (ads plus markdowns). That is survivable, and you will have learned whether you have a buying edge.
- **Confidence: 6/10.** It's a real edge (ads + speed + AI pricing) in the part of the hobby where the money actually is. But it's labor-heavy, lumpy, and depends on local lead quality we haven't measured yet.

## mindset-3: Shop Growth Engine (live inventory + local ads for independent card shops)

- **One-liner:** A productized monthly service for independent local card shops. Claude builds and maintains a searchable "what's in stock" page and Google Business posts, and the user runs local Meta/Google ads to fill release events and tournaments and to bring in walk-in sellers.
- **The hack:** There are about 6,894 trading card stores in North America, and **3,509 are single-owner** ([Rentech Digital, Apr 2026](https://rentechdigital.com/smartscraper/business-report-details/list-of-trading-card-stores-in-north-america)). One review of about 5,700 US local game stores found that **about two in three publish nothing online about their stock** ([GeekVibesNation, via search snippet](https://geekvibesnation.com/local-card-shop-inventory/)). Shops are closing and cite online competition ([SCD closings tag](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/tag/sports-card-shop-closing/)). Their unfair asset is the physical community: events, tournaments and in-person buying. Their weak spot is exactly the user's skill, getting found online and filling events. Claude makes the "inventory page plus weekly posts plus ad creative" work nearly free to deliver. **This sits beside the hobby without inventory risk.** It also feeds mindset-2: shops become the buyers for bulk and junk leads, and the user earns a referral fee.
- **Why now:**
  - Pokemon 30th anniversary waves (Oct 30 and Nov 6, 2026) bring walk-in demand spikes that shops can't catch without online visibility.
  - Riftbound and One Piece growth means new tournament scenes to fill.
  - Whatnot's Shopify integration (around 30 businesses had done $10M+ through it since launch, as of April 2026) shows shops going online need help ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/live-shopping-platform-whatnot-taps-shopify-to-reach-millions-of-merchants-as-it-chases-growth/); [VAR](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/whatnot-shopify-integration/)).
- **How Claude runs it:**
  - **Prospecting.** It builds the prospect list (shops by metro, with or without an inventory page, review counts, ad activity via AdWhispr) and writes a personalized "here's your free event page" demo for each shop as a hosted Artifact.
  - **Inventory pages.** After signup, it maintains the inventory page from the shop's CSV/POS export or TCGplayer store, using an Artifact with a shared database.
  - **Content.** It writes weekly Google Business posts and event announcements.
  - **Ads.** It builds ad creative with Adobe/Higgsfield and launches $10-20/day local campaigns.
  - **Reporting.** It sends a monthly report on a Routine.
- **User's tasks:**
  - Sales calls and in-person visits to local shops (an edge: walk in with a demo on a phone).
  - Collect the shop's inventory export each week, or teach the shop to email it.
  - Approve ad spend.
  - About 2-3 hours a month per shop after setup.
- **First 30 days:**
  - **Day 1-5.** Claude builds 15 demo pages for shops within driving distance.
  - **Day 6-15.** The user visits 10 shops in person and calls 20. Offer the first month free on results ("we fill your next prerelease or you don't pay").
  - **Day 16-30.** Sign 2 pilot shops, run their 30th-anniversary event ads, and record before/after attendance.
- **Path to the goal (math):**
  - **Price:** $600/mo, or $400 + $3 per tracked event registrant. Ad spend is paid by the shop.
  - **Volume:** 1-2 new shops a month (local plus remote via Zoom), with about 5% monthly churn.
  - **Margin:** about 85%.
  - **Honest middle at month 12:** 8 shops × $600 = **$4,800/mo**.
  - **Downside:** 3-4 shops, so about $2,000/mo.
  - **Upside:** 15+ shops plus rev-share on collection-lead referrals. Productize it and hire a VA through Upwork for onboarding.
- **Biggest risk:** Shops run on about 20% card margins and many are cash-tight, so their willingness to pay is unproven (research gap). Expect price pushback and churn. Mitigate with performance-linked pricing and by targeting shops that already run events.
- **Kill rule:** If fewer than 2 of 30 shops pitched convert to paid by Dec 15, 2026, stop. Keep any live clients as a side income.
- **Confidence: 5/10.** The need is real and the cost to deliver is near zero with Claude, but local shops are price-sensitive and this is the slowest of the three to scale.

## Fear-setting (applies to all three)

- **Worst case:**
  - mindset-1: 6 weeks of outreach with no clients. You lose about 30 hours.
  - mindset-2: about $2,500 lost and 3 months of weekends.
  - mindset-3: about 20 hours of shop visits.
  None of these is fatal.
- **Prevent it:** Use the hard caps and dated kill rules above. Never touch the existing PPC client hours. Make no inventory buys outside mindset-2's bankroll, and buy **no modern sealed product to hold**.
- **Repair it:** Fold the learnings into the PPC business. Sell leftover inventory to shops at 60-70% of market.
- **Cost of doing nothing:**
  - 6 months: Q4 2026, the biggest hobby retail season of the decade, passes with no position.
  - 1 year: still a generalist freelancer competing on price.
  - 3 years: the boom has likely corrected (the 2022 pattern). Service and toll businesses survive it, and late entrants to speculation don't.

## Overall verdict

**Conditional yes.** An AI-leveraged card business is +EV for this user only on the **toll-collector side** (ads and services for hobby businesses) or with a **sourcing edge they control** (ad-driven collection buying under a hard bankroll cap). Buying modern product, flipping, grade-and-flip and breaks are -EV or roughly minimum wage for a newcomer in late-cycle 2026. The real danger is not the market. It is the user abandoning a working PPC engine for the hobby's dopamine, so lead with the home-field move and make every other bet small, dated and killable.
