## 1. Flipping and arbitrage (singles and sealed): fee schedules, buy sources, net margin

### Takeaway
After platform fees (about 10–16%), shipping and supplies, a typical raw-single flip bought at about 70% of market value nets about 12–15% of the sale price, which is about 15–20% return on cost. On low-dollar singles under about $5, fixed per-order fees can take 25–30%+. The cheapest exits are Fanatics Collect (6%, or 0% if paid in FanCash), Courtyard (0% seller fee) and Mercari (flat 10%). eBay (about 13.25% + $0.30/0.40) and TCGplayer (10.75% + 2.5% + $0.30) cost the most. Buying from Japan got worse after the 15% tariff and the end of de minimis in August 2025.

### Cited Findings
**eBay**
- Trading cards final value fee (FVF): 13.25% on the sale amount up to $7,500 and 2.35% above $7,500, for sellers with no store or a Starter store. The fee is charged on item price + shipping + sales tax, plus $0.30 per order (orders ≤$10) or $0.40 (orders >$10). — [Synder eBay FVF](https://synder.com/fees-ebay/final-value-fee/); [underpriced.app 2026 guide](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/ebay-fees-complete-guide-2026); [Margeo, "updated July 2026"](https://margeoapp.com/en/blog/ebay-seller-fees-explained/)
- eBay promotion: 50% off the 13.25% FVF on trading-card singles sold for $1,000+ (on the portion up to $7,500). The promo URL has a 2025 path. One 2026 aggregator calls it "standing", but I could not confirm it is still active in Oct 2026. — [eBay promo page](https://pages.ebay.com/promo/2025/tc-singles); [tcgdropradar eBay calculator](https://tcgdropradar.com/tools/ebay-fee-calculator/)
- eBay Live: Live fee rates now apply only to purchases made during the event. Orders placed afterwards pay standard rates. eBay has also tightened repack rules so that only vetted live sellers can sell repacks. — [Value Added Resource, eBay Live fees](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/new-ebay-live-fee-rates); [VAR, eBay repack rules](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-trading-card-repack-rules-live/) (exact eBay Live % not captured, see Gaps)

**Whatnot**
- Baseline: 8% commission + payment processing of about 2.9% + $0.30. Worked example: a $30 item with $5 shipping and $3 tax pays $2.40 commission and $1.40 processing, which is $3.80 total, or 12.6% of the item price. Processing is charged on the full amount the buyer pays. — [closo Whatnot fees 2026](https://closo.co/blogs/fees/whatnot-fees); [closo Whatnot vs eBay 2026](https://closo.co/blogs/fees/the-real-cost-of-selling-whatnot-fees-vs-ebay-fees-in-2026)
- No commission on the part of an item's price above $1,500 in trading cards, sports singles, comics, toys and coins. — [closo](https://closo.co/blogs/fees/whatnot-seller-fees); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/f8e7f7ff-c3fa-4bb3-b315-4f0d20aa6c34)
- **New tiered commission (announced about Sept 17, 2026; effective Sept 21, 2026 for existing sellers).** The rate is set by sales in each four-week period and applies to the next four weeks. Sports/TCG: Standard 8.00%; Tier 1 7.75% (starts at $15,000–$34,999 per four weeks per one source); then 7.00%, 6.75%, 6.50%, 6.25% and 6.00% at the highest public tier. Rates "as low as 3%" are custom deals for the very largest sellers who work with account managers. **Conflict:** one snippet listed tier thresholds in A$ (A$10k/20k/35k/70k/100k/130k). These may be the Australian schedule. The US thresholds need checking on Whatnot's help page. — [Tubefilter Sept 17 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/09/17/whatnot-new-commission-rates-lower-three-percent/); [ChannelX Sept 2026](https://channelx.world/2026/09/whatnot-commission-rates-cut-for-high-volume-sellers/); [VAR, Whatnot cuts fees](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/whatnot-cuts-fees-high-volume-sellers/); [Whatnot help: seller fees](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/4847069165965-Whatnot-seller-fees)

**TCGplayer**
- Level 1–4 sellers: 10.75% marketplace commission (raised from 10.25% on Feb 10, 2026) + 2.5% + $0.30 transaction fee. Pro sellers: 9.25% + 2.5% Pro fee + the same transaction fee. Commission and the Pro fee are capped at $75 per item (raised from $50). — [TCGplayer help: Fees](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/201357836-Fees); [Fee cap FAQ](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/37531606328727)
- TCGplayer Direct (from June 18, 2026): cards under $2.49 pay a 50% card-value fee (no marketplace commission). Cards above $2.49 pay $1.12 per card plus standard fees. Shipping Replacement Costs were removed. — [TCGplayer seller blog](https://seller.tcgplayer.com/blog/simpler-and-more-predictable-fees-coming-for-tcgplayer-direct-june-18)

**Fanatics Collect / Fanatics Live**
- Marketplace Buy Now: 6% seller fee if the list price is below 120% of market value, 12% if at or above 120%. — [Fanatics Collect support: Buy Now fees](https://support.fanaticscollect.com/en_us/buy-now-fees-ry33QCXaxe.md); [VAR/nifty summaries](https://www.nifty.ai/post/best-site-to-sell-sports-cards)
- "FanCash Payouts": no transaction fee if the seller takes payment in FanCash rather than cash. — [SI Collectibles](https://www.si.com/collectibles/fanatics-collect-eliminates-seller-fees-new-fancash-payouts-program)
- Weekly auctions: seller receives 100% of the hammer price, with 2–15% bonuses on cards that sell for $50+. The buyer premium funds this. — [Fanatics Live blog](https://about.fanatics.live/post/selling-your-sports-cards-quick-easy-way); [nifty.ai](https://www.nifty.ai/post/best-site-to-sell-sports-cards)

**Mercari, COMC, Courtyard**
- Mercari: flat 10% selling fee on item + buyer-paid shipping. Since Jan 6, 2025 this includes payment processing. $3 for Instant Pay, otherwise free payout. — [closo Mercari fees](https://closo.co/blogs/platform-specific-guides/mercari-fees); [underpriced.app](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/mercari-fees-explained-complete-guide-2026)
- COMC: 5% transaction fee + 10% cash-out fee + intake fee per card: Standard $0.65/card (100-card minimum, about 16 weeks), Select $1, Elite $2 (about 2 weeks). Net recovery is typically about 70–80%. Sources are secondary and the dates are uncertain. — [geekydomain](https://geekydomain.com/blogs/guides/how-to-sell-your-tcg-singles-for-max-value); [pregradecards](https://pregradecards.com/blog/cardsource-vs-comc-canadian-alternative-2026)
- Courtyard (tokenized, vaulted graded cards; Brink's vault): no seller fee, no buyer fee, no storage fee, a 90% fair-market-value buyback guarantee on its packs, and physical redemption on demand. Source is a review site, not Courtyard's own terms. — [thecardshopfinder Courtyard review](https://thecardshopfinder.com/guides/pack-ripping/courtyard-io-review/); [Privy blog](https://privy.io/blog/inside-the-stack-how-courtyard-is-reinventing-the-collectibles-marketplace)

**Buy sources**
- Japan imports: 15% baseline US tariff on Japanese goods (US–Japan deal, retroactive to Aug 7, 2025). De minimis ended Aug 29, 2025, so every parcel is dutiable and carriers add $5–$30 processing fees. Example: a JP booster box at $78 + $15 shipping + $13.95 tariff = $106.95, against $130–150 for the English equivalent. A $20 single can pick up a $3 tariff plus a $15 processing fee, nearly doubling its cost. JP cards trade at a 15–40% premium to English, but tariffs erode much of it. — [CardCodex, US tariffs and Pokemon](https://cardcodex.com/blog/us-tariffs-influence-pokemon-card-hobby/)
- Retail sealed: Target expects >$1B in annual trading-card sales, with category revenue up about 70% (2025). Walmart Marketplace online card sales rose 200% from Feb 2024 to Jun 2025. — [Target corporate, Sept 2025](https://corporate.target.com/news-features/article/2025/09/trading-cards); [inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/target-leads-with-pokemon-and-sports-card-sales-spike-in-2025)
- Sealed spreads: a Pokémon booster box with $144 MSRP "regularly trades for $160–$220" on the secondary market (aggregator claim, set not specified). — [closo, resell Pokemon 2026](https://closo.co/blogs/blog/how-to-resell-pokemon)

### Inferences
- **(calc) Fee take per $100 raw single:**
  - eBay: 13.25% × ($100 + $5 shipping charged + about $8.40 tax) = $15.03, plus $0.40 = **$15.43, or 15.4% of the item price**.
  - Whatnot standard: 8% × $100 = $8.00, plus processing of 2.9% × about $113 + $0.30 = $3.58, total **about $11.58 (11.6%)**. At Tier 3 (6.75%) it is about $10.33.
  - TCGplayer Level 1–4: $10.75 + 2.5% × about $105 + $0.30 = **about $13.70 (13.7%)**.
  - Mercari: **$10.50 (10.5%)**.
  - Fanatics Collect at market price: **$6 (6%)**, or $0 if paid in FanCash.
  - Courtyard: **$0**.
- **(calc) Low-dollar penalty.** A $2 TCGplayer card pays $0.215 + $0.05 + $0.30 = **$0.57, or 28% of the sale**, before a stamp or envelope. A $2 card sold through Direct pays 50% ($1.00). Bulk singles under about $3 are close to worthless to flip one at a time.
- **(calc) Typical flip.** Buy a $100-market card at 70% ($70) from a show, Facebook Marketplace or a collection. Sell on eBay for $100 + $5 shipping charged. Net = $105 − $15.43 fees − about $5 actual postage (assumed) − about $0.50 supplies − $70 = **about $14 profit (14% of sale; 20% return on cost)**. The same card on Fanatics Collect at market price nets about $23.50. The choice of channel is worth about 9 points of margin.
- **(calc) Japan import.** The $106.95 landed JP box against a $130–150 English price looks like a 22–40% spread. But JP and English boxes are different products with different contents, and resale still pays 11–15% in fees plus shipping. The real net is probably 5–20% per box, before currency moves and the carrier fee on small parcels.
- Buying cheaper (at 50–60% of market, from collections or estate buys) matters much more than which channel you sell on. That is where the money is.

## 2. Grading arbitrage (buy raw → grade → sell graded)

### Takeaway
The grading market in 2026 is limited by capacity. PSA paused all Value tiers on June 2, 2026, leaving Regular at $79.99/card as the cheapest PSA option. Beckett and TAG also restricted cheap tiers. CGC Bulk ($17, 150 working days) and SGC are the cheap options left. At $80/card the math only works on cards where a PSA 10 is worth several hundred dollars more than raw. At Value pricing ($25–33) the math is positive mainly on high-multiple modern Pokémon with roughly 50%+ raw-to-10 odds on carefully pre-screened cards. Even then the expected return is often only low single-digit to about 20% ROI, tied up for 2–6 months.

### Cited Findings
- PSA 2026 prices (after a 2026 increase): Value Bulk $24.99 (was $21.99), Value $32.99 (was $27.99), Value Plus $49.99 (was $44.99), Value Max $64.99 (was $59.99). Turnaround estimates were extended: Value Plus 45 business days, Value Max 35, Regular 25. — [allvintagecards, PSA hikes 2026](https://allvintagecards.com/psa-hikes-grading-costs-and-turnaround-times-2026/); [allvintagecards PSA price chart](https://allvintagecards.com/psa-grading-costs/)
- PSA paused all four Value tiers on June 2, 2026, because the backlog neared 10M cards. The backlog grew after PSA announced a roughly $200M capacity expansion, which brought in a surge of about 1.6M cards. The Value tiers reopen when the backlog reaches about 5M. While they are paused, the cheapest PSA tier is Regular at $79.99. — [JustCollect](https://blog.justcollect.com/psa-pauses-card-grading-on-value-tiers-due-to-demand); [misprint](https://www.misprint.com/posts/how-long-does-psa-grading-take-2026)
- Sept 8, 2026: PSA backlog was 9.9M, down 2.5M from the July peak and the first reading below 10M. PSA said it could evaluate reopening one Value tier before the end of September. The full reopen still requires about 5M. — [PSA Backlog Tracker](https://www.psacard.com/info/backlog-tracker); [thecardshopfinder, PSA under 10 million](https://thecardshopfinder.com/blog/psa-under-10-million/); [SI](https://www.si.com/collectibles/psa-backlog-falls-12-million-means-card-grading-2026)
- CGC (schedule as of Aug 4, 2026): Bulk $17 (150 working days), Economy $20 (90), Standard $55 (10), Express $100 (5), WalkThrough $300 (2), Unlimited $300 + 1% of fair market value. — [cardgrade.io CGC cost](https://cardgrade.io/cgc-card-grading-cost)
- SGC prices by declared value. One source says $15 base for cards up to $1,500 and $85 up to $3,500, with 20–30 business days (35–45 at peak). **Conflict:** another 2026 source says SGC's minimum is $50 (40–50 business days). — [pokemonpricetracker SGC vs PSA 2026](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/blog/posts/sgc-vs-psa-grading-2026-cost-turnaround-analysis); [pregradecards state of submission 2026](https://pregradecards.com/state-of-card-submission-2026); [SI: SGC raises prices](https://www.si.com/collectibles/sgc-latest-company-raise-grading-prices)
- Beckett (BGS): Express $79.95 (15 business days), Priority $124.95 (5). Base and Standard were paused and expected to reopen Sept 15, 2026. — [pregradecards Aug 2026, "PSA & Beckett both paused"](https://pregradecards.com/blog/psa-beckett-both-paused-where-to-grade-cards-august-2026)
- TAG: cheapest available was $150 (5 business days). Regular tiers closed at capacity in mid-2026, leaving only Limited Priority and Walkthrough. — [misprint grading costs compared 2026](https://www.misprint.com/posts/pokemon-card-grading-costs-compared-2026); [tcgtalk](https://tcgtalk.com/guides/best-tcg-card-grading-company-2026)
- Volume: 26.8M cards were graded in 2025 across the major graders, up 32% from about 20M in 2024. PSA graded 71.8% (19.26M). — [CLLCT](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/sports-cards/major-authenticators-graded-more-than-26-million-cards-in-2025); [SI](https://www.si.com/collectibles/over-26-million-cards-graded-in-2025-how-the-market-exploded)
- Gem (10) rates for H1 2025: 43% across all graders and 43% at PSA (8.9M cards). TCG (Pokémon and similar) gemmed **50%** of 7.2M. Sports cards gemmed **34%** of 5.1M. These rates are for cards submitters already screened, not for random raw cards. — [CLLCT, gem rate 43%](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/sports-cards/how-many-cards-grade-as-10s-gem-rate-at-43-so-far-in-2025); [GemRate monthly recaps](https://www.gemrate.com/november-2025-recap); [pokemonpricetracker pop guide](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/blog/posts/pokemon-card-population-report-guide-rarity-analysis-2026)
- Modern PSA 10 vs PSA 9 premiums have narrowed. Typical multiples: English modern chase card 1.3–2.0x; modern alt art/SIR 1.5–2.5x; Japanese SAR/SIR 1.4–2.2x; high-population promos 1.1–1.5x. Causes are better print quality and PSA 10 populations in the tens of thousands. — [pokemonpricetracker PSA 10 vs 9 2026](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/blog/posts/psa-10-vs-psa-9-is-the-price-gap-worth-it-in-2026)
- Example from an aggregator: a raw card bought for $8 that grades PSA 10 can sell for $60–$100, but grading costs $25–$50 per card and takes 20–65 business days. — [closo, resell Pokemon 2026](https://closo.co/blogs/blog/how-to-resell-pokemon)

### Inferences
- **(calc) EV template, one card.** EV = Σ pᵢ × Vᵢ × (1 − f) − s − (raw cost + grading fee + inbound shipping and insurance per card).
  - Here f is the selling fee (about 0.154 on eBay, 0.06 on Fanatics Collect) and s is outbound shipping (about $5, assumed).
- **Scenario A: modern Pokémon SIR at PSA Value ($32.99), selling on eBay.**
  - Inputs: raw $40; PSA 10 = $150; PSA 9 = $50; PSA ≤8 = $35. Probabilities are p10 = 0.50 (the TCG gem rate), p9 = 0.40, p≤8 = 0.10. Inbound shipping and insurance about $3/card (assumed).
  - Expected sale = 75 + 20 + 3.5 = $98.50. Net after 15.4% fees and $5 shipping = **$78.30**.
  - Cost = 40 + 32.99 + 3 = **$75.99**. **EV ≈ +$2.31 per card (3% ROI) over about 2–3 months.**
  - On Fanatics Collect (6%) the EV is about +$11.60 (15%).
- **Scenario B: the same card at PSA Regular ($79.99), the only PSA option while Value is paused.** Cost $122.99 against the same $78.30 net. **EV ≈ −$45 per card.**
- **Scenario C: sports card at a 34% gem rate.** Inputs: raw $60, PSA 10 $300, PSA 9 $90, PSA ≤8 $55; p10 = 0.34, p9 = 0.50, p≤8 = 0.16. Expected sale = 102 + 45 + 8.8 = $155.80. eBay net = $126.80.
  - At Value: cost $96, **EV +$30.80 (32%)**.
  - At Regular: cost $143, **EV −$16**.
- **Rule of thumb (calc).** At a cheap tier (about $20–35 all-in), you need a PSA 10 worth at least about 3–4x raw and a realistic gem chance of at least 40%. At $80, you need a PSA 10 premium over raw of at least about $250.
- The 43–50% gem rates are overstated for a buyer of random raw cards. They describe cards people chose to submit, and the cards that miss (9s and lower) are what drag EV negative. So "positive math" depends on screening skill, not luck.
- Capacity constraint: with PSA's backlog at about 10M and cheap tiers closed, grading arbitrage is effectively shut for small operators at PSA until the Value tiers reopen. CGC Bulk ($17, 150 working days, about 7 months) is cheap but locks capital for a long time.

### Gaps
- Could not verify PSA's live service page or whether a Value tier reopened in late Sept 2026.
- SGC's 2026 base price conflicts ($15 vs $50).
- No verified per-card PSA 10/raw price pairs collected from sold listings. The scenarios use assumed values.
- No large-sample data on what share of grading submitters profit. Only anecdotes exist, and they suffer from survivor bias.

## 3. Live breaking (Whatnot, Fanatics Live, YouTube)

### Takeaway
Breakers sell spots for about 1.2–1.5x the product cost, and some hype breaks go far higher. Whatnot takes about 8% plus about 3% processing (lower at volume from Sept 21, 2026). That leaves roughly 10–20% of revenue as gross margin on a fairly priced break. Profit depends on volume, getting product at distributor or allocation cost, and stream labor. Legal risk is real and rising. Arbitration claims grew from 15 to about 70 claimants, plus a separate lawsuit and an unsealed California False Claims Act suit naming Whatnot and Fanatics Live. eBay has limited repacks to vetted sellers.

### Cited Findings
- In most breaks, total spot revenue exceeds the product cost by 20–50%. Hype example: a $10,000 case of Prizm Football sold as $30,000 of spots (200–300% markup). Buyer expected value is about 70–85% of spot price in a well-run break and about 30–40% in a predatory one. The suggested pricing for pick-your-team breaks is box cost × 1.3–1.5 to cover Whatnot's 8%, about 3% processing, supplies and profit. — [thecardshopfinder breaks guide](https://thecardshopfinder.com/guides/sports-cards/card-breaks-guide/); [cardpulse, breaks explained 2026](https://www.cardpulse.club/blog/card-breaks-explained-2026); [The Hobby Wire, "Before You Break"](https://thehobbywire.substack.com/p/before-you-break)
- In a random team break, a buyer's expected value is the room average (total room value divided by the number of teams), which is "reliably below the EV of the box". — [cardpulse](https://www.cardpulse.club/blog/card-breaks-explained-2026)
- Whatnot scale: $359M revenue in 2024 (+102% year over year) on about $3B GMV. More than $8B in GMV in 2025. More than 6M trading cards sold per month. Valued at about $5B after its Jan 2025 Series E. — [Sacra, Whatnot at $359M/yr](https://sacra.com/research/whatnot-at-359m-yr/); [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/new-legal-action-suggests-whatnot-is-unlawful-gambling-scheme/)
- Seller distribution (Whatnot's own "State of Livestream Selling" reporting, so self-selected): 500+ sellers at $1M+ annualized sales; "66% of sellers earn more than $10k/month" and "one in four make over $300k annually". These figures probably describe a surveyed group of active sellers, not all sellers. Backyard Breaks is named as one of the biggest breakers. — [ecommercebonsai Whatnot stats](https://ecommercebonsai.com/whatnot-statistics/); [VAR, Whatnot $2B GMV report](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/whatnot-2-billion-gmv-state-of-livestream-selling-report/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whatnot)
- Legal: attorney Paul Lesko filed arbitration demands for 15 customers in spring (2025/2026), alleging that randomized breaks violate California's ban on illegal lotteries. By June the count was nearly 70 claimants, with a separate lawsuit by other plaintiffs. The claimants spent a combined $252M+ on Whatnot, and the complaints call it "an unregulated online casino". A newly unsealed 2025 California False Claims Act suit names Whatnot and Fanatics Live over unlicensed box-break lotteries and unpaid taxes. Whatnot's response: "Gambling isn't allowed on Whatnot." — [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/whatnot-arbitration-claims-breaking-practices/); [Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/whatnot-arbitration-filing-lesko/); [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/new-legal-action-suggests-whatnot-is-unlawful-gambling-scheme/); [SCD](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/whatnot-lawsuit-could-mean-big-trouble-for-breakers-help-protect-collectors); [thecardshopfinder, breaks and repacks legal scrutiny](https://thecardshopfinder.com/blog/breaks-repacks-legal-scrutiny/)
- eBay restricted trading-card repack sales to vetted live sellers. — [VAR](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-trading-card-repack-rules-live/)
- Consolidation: in 2024 the competitors were Whatnot, Fanatics Live, eBay Live, Goldin streams and Discord breakers. By 2026 the market has consolidated, with Fanatics positioned on high-value breaks and auctions. — [cardpulse, Whatnot vs Fanatics Collect 2026](https://www.cardpulse.club/blog/whatnot-vs-fanatics-collect-2026-market)

### Inferences
- **(calc) One pick-your-team break.** Product $1,000; 30 spots; 1.4x markup gives **$1,400 revenue**.
  - Whatnot 8% commission = $112.
  - Processing at 2.9% on about $1,512 including tax = $43.85, plus 30 × $0.30 = $9, so $52.85.
  - Supplies (top loaders, team bags, mailers) at about $1/spot = $30 (assumed). Buyers usually pay shipping (assumed).
  - **Net ≈ $205 per break (14.6% of revenue).** At Whatnot Tier 3 (6.75%) it is about $222.
- **(calc) The same break at 1.2x markup ($1,200)** nets about $1,200 − $1,000 − $96 − $45 − $30 = **about $29**. At a 1.2x markup one break barely breaks even, so breakers depend on volume, hype pricing or buying product below secondary-market price.
- **(calc) Scale.** A breaker doing $100k of four-week GMV at about 15% net margin makes about $15k gross profit per four weeks. That is before labor (hosts, shipping staff), studio costs and slow-selling spots the breaker has to cover. Slow spots are the main way breakers lose money: they buy the spot themselves or sell it at a discount.
- Breaking is the business model under the most legal risk. If a California ruling or settlement finds random breaks to be lotteries, random-team formats (the highest-margin format) could be restricted. Pick-your-team breaks with fixed prices look less exposed.
- Saturation shows in the consolidation and in the fee cuts to keep volume sellers (Whatnot vs eBay Live). The fee cuts help big operators most (6% vs 8%), so they widen the gap between large and small breakers.

### Gaps
- No verified interview or published figures on any individual breaker's net income (Backyard Breaks, Layton, etc.). Search returned none.
- No confirmed Fanatics Live breaker commission.
- No data on the share of breakers that quit or lose money.
- The status of the Lesko arbitrations and the California False Claims Act suit as of Oct 2026 was not confirmed.

## 4. Card shops, vending machines, show tables, consignment, bulk and buylist

### Takeaway
These are all lower-margin "picks and shovels" retail plays. Card margins at shops have fallen to about 20% after the pandemic, while accessories earn 65–70%. Vending machine economics come almost entirely from vendor marketing (35–55% net margin, $600–2,500/month revenue per machine) and should be heavily discounted. The official Pokémon machines (about 1,473 locations by mid-2025) are not for sale. Show tables cost $40 to $2,500. Bulk buying spreads are a few cents per card.

### Cited Findings
- Card shops: card margins were "100 to 400% during the pandemic" and now sit "around the market price", giving about 20% profit. Accessories (sleeves, top loaders, binders) earn 65–70% gross margin, the highest in the business. — search snippet attributed to card-shop coverage ([AOL/Taunton Gazette, Taunton Pokémon shop](https://www.aol.com/fairy-tale-story-taunton-pokemon-102416007.html); [VMFS 2026 guide](https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/pokemon-card-vending-machine-the-complete-2026-operator-guide)). The exact attribution between these two pages is unverified.
- Vending (vendor-sourced, optimistic): one well-placed Pokémon machine makes $600–$2,500/month in revenue. Profit is said to be $750–$1,800+/month with a 4–7 month ROI. At 5 transactions/day it nets about $963/month after wholesale cost and fees; at 10–15/day about $1,900–$2,900. Net margin is 35–55% after product, placement fees and restocking. — [VMFS USA, is it profitable](https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/is-the-pokemon-vending-machine-business-profitable); [VTM profit calculator](https://vtmvending.com/pages/pokemon-vending-machine-profit-calculator); [VTM profitability blog](https://vtmvending.com/blogs/the-automated-retailer/trading-card-vending-machine-profitability)
- A Miami hobby shop reported an extra $5,000/month in revenue after adding a Pokémon vending machine. This came from a syndicated **press release**, likely paid placement. — [Watauga Democrat press release](https://www.wataugademocrat.com/online_features/press_releases/local-hobby-shop-sees-5-000-revenue-boost-after-adding-pok-mon-card-vending-machine/article_a1009013-40b5-50d4-8835-eaf21e38ae52.html)
- Official TPCi vending machines grew from 45 units (2023) to about 1,473 locations (mid-2025). TPCi says they are "owned and operated by The Pokémon Company International and there are no plans to sell them." Independent machines run in gas stations, malls and shops. — [VMFS 2026 guide](https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/pokemon-card-vending-machine-the-complete-2026-operator-guide); [ScreenCrush](https://screencrush.com/ixp/252/p/pokemon-card-vending-machines/)
- Show tables: small regional shows (100–300 tables) charge $75–$200 per 8-ft table; mid-size shows (500–1,500 tables) $200–$500; major shows such as the National $500–$2,500. Plan on a total show cost of 2–4x the table fee. A real local example: Hightstown, NJ show at $40/table ($70 for two). — [thecardshopfinder, sell at card shows](https://thecardshopfinder.com/guides/card-shows/sell-at-card-shows/); [booth kit guide](https://thecardshopfinder.com/blog/card-show-booth-kit-vendor-complete-guide/); [Mercer County NJ event](https://visit.mercercountynj.gov/event/hightstowns-sports-card-collectibles-show/)
- Show inventory: dealers suggest $2,500–$5,000 of starting inventory for a table. — [allvintagecards, flipping at card shows](https://allvintagecards.com/flipping-card-shows/)
- Consignment: COMC net recovery is about 70–80% after fees (see section 1). Fanatics Collect auctions pay 100% of hammer plus a 2–15% bonus (see section 1).
- Bulk: retail bulk lots sell for $25–60 per 1,000 cards (junk commons $0.02–0.04/card; premium bulk with rares/holos $0.10–0.50). Store buy rates: Knight and Day Games pays $8 per 1,000 in cash or $10 in store credit. Otakumart (**New Zealand, NZD**) pays $10 per 1,000. Super rares from some eras get $350 per 1,000. — [pokemonpricetracker, card cost](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/pokemon-cards-cost); [Knight and Day bulk rates](https://knightanddaygames.com/pages/sell-us-your-bulk); [Otakumart](https://otakumart.co.nz/pages/sell-your-cards)
- Market size context: Pokémon is reportedly more than 68% of US hobby retail card sales. The Pokémon Company FY2025 revenue was reportedly $2.9B (+38%). Both come from an aggregator snippet, not checked against primary sources. — [closo](https://closo.co/blogs/blog/how-to-resell-pokemon)

### Inferences
- **(calc) Bulk.** Buy at $8–10 per 1,000 and sell picked or sorted lots at $25–60 per 1,000, less about 13% fees and shipping on roughly 3–4 lb (assumed $10–15). That leaves about $0–30 gross per 1,000 cards, before sorting labor of perhaps 1–2 hours per 1,000. This is close to minimum wage or below **unless** you find valuable cards in the bulk ("picking"). Picking is the real business model.
- **(calc) Vending, discounted.** Take the vendor's 5 transactions/day case ($963/month net) and remove theft, downtime, restocking driving and MSRP sourcing problems (product shortages at retail). A realistic range is probably $300–$900/month per machine. Payback depends on machine cost, which I could not verify. Competition from the official TPCi machines (about 1,473+) caps placement at big-box retailers.
- **(calc) Show table.** A $150 regional table × 3 = $450 all-in. Flipping $3,000 of inventory at 20–30% gross margin gives $600–$900 gross, so **$150–$450 net for a day plus prep**. Profit depends on buying at the table (from walk-up sellers) more than on selling.
- A physical shop's survival depends on accessories, events (TCG tournaments) and buylist spreads, not on margins from sealed product.

## 5. Software and data businesses serving collectors

### Takeaway
There is a proven but modest market. CollX has about $10M in annual revenue from about 4.5M users (500k monthly actives). Collectr is a bootstrapped "eight-figure" business. Ludex claims 3M+ users. Prices cluster at $5–25/month. The platforms (Whatnot $359M revenue in 2024; PSA/Collectors) capture far more value than the tools do.

### Cited Findings
- CollX: about 4.5M users, 500k monthly actives, 1–2M card scans per day, about $15M raised (including $10M in March 2025), roughly $10M annual revenue and described as profitable. Pricing: Pro $10/month or $100/year; Gold $24.99/month or $249/year. — [TechCrunch, Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/07/collx-raises-10m-to-grow-its-card-collection-marketplace); [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/entrepreneurship/collx-trading-card-marketplace-profitable/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/collx-sports-card-scanner/id1581164444)
- Collectr: bootstrapped into "an eight-figure business". Pro costs $7.99/month or $59.99/year (unlimited scans, P&L analytics, price history). — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/how-collectr-bootstrapped-a-trading-card-hobby-into-an-eight-figure-business/)
- Card Ladder Pro: $19.99/month or $199.99/year. **Conflict:** another source lists $9.99/month. — [App Store: Card Ladder](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1541535971); [screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/card-ladder)

The subscriber claim comes from an aggregator and looks high; treat it as unverified. — [apppricinglab Ludex](https://apppricinglab.com/iap/apple/1616691213); [stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/en/ludex)

### Inferences
- **(calc)** CollX's ~$10M revenue on 4.5M users is about $2.20 per user per year. If most of it is subscriptions at about $100/year, that is roughly 100k paying users, about 2% of users or 20% of monthly actives. That is a reasonable benchmark for a new entrant.
- The space is crowded (CollX, Ludex, Collectr, Card Ladder, Market Movers, plus PSA's own app). Scanning and pricing has become a commodity. A new tool would need a distinct edge, such as AI pre-grade screening, which bears directly on grading EV in section 2, or cross-listing automation.

## 7. Evidence of actual operator earnings (and survivor bias)

### Takeaway
Hard, verified earnings data is scarce. Most public "how much I made" evidence is anecdotal and small: $230 profit on $100 of bulk at one show, $500 at a first show, $130 on five flips. Platform-level statistics come from Whatnot's self-reported surveys. Tax considerations (inventory accounting and self-employment tax) further reduce net returns. Reddit could not be accessed directly.

### Cited Findings
- Show flipping anecdotes: $100 of bulk turned into $330 ($230 profit) at one show; $500 made at a first six-hour show, with later shows weaker; five cards flipped for $130 profit. — [allvintagecards, flipping at a local show](https://allvintagecards.com/flipping-card-shows/); [Ball Card Genius substack](https://ballcardgenius.substack.com/p/flipping-5-cards-for-130-profit-making)
- An aggregator says "for most people who try it, the reality looks a lot more like unsold cards than a highlight-reel resale story". It also cites a case of $1,500 turned into $3,800 overnight on mispriced graded cards, which is anecdotal and promotional. — [pokemonpricetracker flipping 2026](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/blog/posts/how-to-flip-pokemon-cards-for-profit-2026-strategy)
- Taxes: a US forum thread estimates total taxes (federal, state, self-employment, sales, license) take about 50% of net profit for a business reseller. This is a forum opinion, not tax advice. — [Elite Fourum](https://elitefourum.com/t/help-with-tax-questions-in-regards-to-pokemon-based-in-us/30564)
- Whatnot's self-reported seller outcomes (500+ sellers at $1M+; "66% earn >$10k/month") come from surveys of active sellers. — [ecommercebonsai](https://ecommercebonsai.com/whatnot-statistics/)
- Lesko's arbitration claimants say they lost money while spending a combined $252M+ on Whatnot. This shows how much buyers lose to breakers. — [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/new-legal-action-suggests-whatnot-is-unlawful-gambling-scheme/)

### Inferences
- **Survivor bias is severe.** Posted earnings come from people who succeeded, and platform statistics are self-reported by active sellers. Sellers who failed or stopped do not post. A realistic EV model should assume that the median part-time flipper earns about $5–20 per hour of labor after fees, before taxes, and that a meaningful share lose money in their first year (no quantitative source; this is an inference).
- **(calc) Part-time flipper EV.** 10 hours/week; $3,000 of capital turning over about once every 6 weeks (about 8.7 turns per year), so about $26k of annual sales. At 14% net margin (section 1 math) that is about $3,640/year, or **about $7/hour**, before taxes. Doubling the margin through better buying (collections at 50–60% of market) gives about $14/hour.
- Expected earnings per hour rise with capital and buying advantage (relationships, show networks, the 0–6% fee channels), not with listing effort.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/sportscards, r/PokemonTCG, r/pkmntcgcollections, r/flipping) could not be accessed directly. Search did not return specific "year in review" P&L threads with numbers.
- No podcast transcripts or interviews with dollar figures for dealers or breakers were found.
- No dataset on the failure or exit rate of card resellers.
