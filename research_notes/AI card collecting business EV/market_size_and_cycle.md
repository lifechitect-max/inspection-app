# Trading Card Collectibles Market: Size, Growth and Cycle (as of 2026-10-01)

Research method note: about 25 web searches/fetches. Several primary or near-primary sites were blocked by the network proxy for full-page fetch (cllct.com, sportscollectorsdaily.com, athlonsports.com, seller.tcgplayer.com, boardgamewire.com, cnbc.com). Figures from those sources come from search-result snippets/summaries, not full-page reads, and should be spot-checked before they are relied on. Source quality is flagged inline: [PRIMARY] = company filing, earnings or official release; [TRADE] = hobby/trade press; [WEAK] = vendor market-research estimate, SEO blog or aggregator.

## 1. Total market size (global and US), primary vs secondary

### Takeaway
No credible company-level figure covers the whole market. Vendor estimates for the global TCG/trading-card market range from about $7.9B to $18.5B for 2025, and the US is put at about $5.9B (2025), with the secondary market at roughly half. These are weak estimates. Company data is a better anchor: Fanatics/Topps collectibles was about $1.6B in 2024 and targeted about $2B in 2025; Whatnot did $8B+ total GMV in 2025, mostly cards; eBay's collectibles is its largest growth contributor; GameStop's collectibles run rate is about $1.4B a year. Taken together, the US primary+secondary card economy is plausibly well above the $5.9B vendor number, at least in the low-double-digit billions of transaction value. That is my inference; see below.

### Cited Findings
**Vendor market-size estimates (all [WEAK]; definitions differ and they contradict each other):**
- Global trading card games market valued at $18.5B in 2025 (Grand View Research) — [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/trading-card-games-market-report)
- US trading cards market $5.9B in 2025, projected $6.2B in 2026 and $10.2B by 2033 (7.2% CAGR). Secondary market = 53.6% share in 2025 — [Grand View Research US report](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-trading-cards-market-report)
- Other 2025 global estimates: $14.5B (trading cards), $8.4B (TCG industry), and $7.85B (TCG, Mordor), rising to $12.86B by 2031 — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/trading-card-game-market); [pregradecards industry guide](https://pregradecards.com/blog/trading-card-games-industry-guide-2026); [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/trading-card)

### Inferences
- Vendor TAM figures badly undercount the secondary market. Whatnot alone did $8B+ GMV in 2025 with cards as the majority (see section 4), which already exceeds Grand View's entire US market figure ($5.9B). Add eBay's card GMV (undisclosed but likely several $B), Fanatics Collect, Goldin, Courtyard and TCGplayer, and the US secondary transaction volume is plausibly $10B+ a year. Platform GMV double-counts flips, so it is not "market size" in the strict sense.
- Primary (sealed/new) US+global supply is dominated by three makers. Topps/Fanatics was about $2B in 2025 (target about $3B in 2026). Pokémon prints about 10B cards/yr at maximum capacity, with dollar value not disclosed. Bandai (One Piece, Dragon Ball, etc.) is #3. Panini is shrinking after losing its licenses.

### Gaps
- No primary-source split of US primary vs secondary dollar volume exists. The Grand View 53.6% secondary share is a vendor estimate.
- The Pokémon Company does not publish TCG dollar revenue. The ~10B card count is the best official proxy.
- Collectors Holdings (PSA parent) is private and its revenue was not found.
- Panini revenue was not found.

## 2. Growth drivers 2025–2026 (Pokémon, One Piece, Lorcana, Fanatics/Topps, Panini, Riftbound)

### Takeaway
Growth in 2025–26 is real and broad. eBay focus categories grew 26% YoY in Q2 2026, led by trading cards. Whatnot GMV roughly doubled. GameStop collectibles grew 57%. PSA grading volume set a record (+26%). Pokémon is still supply-constrained at maximum print capacity, and the 30th anniversary (2026) caused instant sellouts. One Piece passed Yu-Gi-Oh for #3. Lorcana has cooled. Riftbound (Riot, launched Oct 2025) is the breakout new TCG. Fanatics/Topps now holds MLB, NBA (from late 2025) and NFL (from 2026-04-01) licenses, and Panini is out.

### Inferences
- Sports cards consolidating under one licensor (Fanatics/Topps for MLB, NBA and NFL from April 2026) means a single company controls primary supply and pricing for US sports. That raises the risk of product overproduction, a junk-wax-style dynamic, in modern sports.
- Pokémon is the opposite case: demand still exceeds a capped supply, and the 30th anniversary keeps retail scarcity acute through Q4 2026. The scarcity is mostly at MSRP retail, though. Secondary prices for modern sealed product are already correcting (section 3).

## 3. Where are we in the boom/bust cycle?

### Takeaway
As of fall 2026 the market is split, not in a uniform bubble. Vintage and true high-end are at records (the $16.49M Pikachu Illustrator in Feb 2026). Modern sports singles are in a mild 12-month correction (Card Ladder: basketball -2%, football -6%, baseball -8%, hockey -11%). Modern Pokémon sealed and singles are 20–50% off their 2025 speculative peaks even as retail sells out. Transaction volumes (eBay, Whatnot, PSA, GameStop) are at all-time highs. That pattern looks like a late-boom phase: record volume, falling prices for mass-produced modern product. It does not look like a 2022-style volume collapse yet.

### Cited Findings
- **Card Ladder sports indexes (12-month, reported mid-2026):** basketball -2%, football -6%, baseball -8.35%, hockey -11%+. Characterized as "a general correction rather than a collapse." The Card Ladder Pokémon Index was +116% over the same kind of 12-month window. Vintage baseball "shows no signs of cooling." Unnumbered base from flagship and breaker-heavy products shows cooling as of July 2026 — [Athlon 2026 mid-year collectibles report](https://athlonsports.com/collectibles/2026-mid-year-collectibles-report-sports-cards-tcg); [Athlon July 2026 market report](https://athlonsports.com/collectibles/july-2026-sports-card-market-report); [Attic Capital sports card market report 2026](https://atticcapital.com/sports-card-market-report/) (snippets only; full pages blocked) [TRADE]
- **Card Ladder Pokémon long-run:** cumulative return of 3,821% from 2004 to August 2025 (another snippet says 6,208% since 2004; the window differs) — [pokemonwizard/pokemonpricetracker summaries](https://www.pokemonwizard.com/stats) [WEAK aggregator; numbers inconsistent]
- **Pokémon correction:** since late 2025, modern chase cards and sealed product have corrected about 20–50% from speculative peaks as supply caught up, while scarce vintage set records — [PokemonPriceTracker "what really happened"](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/blog/posts/pokemon-card-market-crash-2026-what-really-happened) [WEAK]
- **September 2026 modern sealed drops (30-day):** Destined Rivals booster box raw -20%+ (PSA 10 about -15%), Surging Sparks about -18% raw / -13% PSA 10, Journey Together packs about -22%. - **Prior cycle (2020–2023):** the Card Ladder CL50 (blue-chip index) was down 19.8% in 2023. - **"Junk Wax 2.0" debate:** hobby press is openly debating overproduction of modern product — [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/the-junk-wax-2-0-debate-rears-its-ugly-head-again/); "The sports card bubble isn't coming, it's already leaking" — [Jack Chapel Substack](https://jackchapel.substack.com/p/the-sports-card-bubble-isnt-coming) [OPINION]
- **Record sales:** Logan Paul's PSA 10 Pikachu Illustrator sold for $16.49M at Goldin on 2026-02-16 after a 41-day auction, the most expensive trading card ever. ### Inferences
- **Cycle positioning:** In 2021, prices and volumes peaked together. In 2026, volumes and platform growth are at records while prices of mass-print modern product (Pokémon modern sealed, sports base/flagship) are already falling. That fits a late-cycle "supply catching up" phase. The 2022-style break would come if volumes also roll over, which would show up as Whatnot growth slowing, PSA monthly volumes declining, or eBay focus-category growth decelerating. No such rollover is visible in data through Q2 2026.
- **Junk-wax parallel (1987–1994, background knowledge, not sourced in this pass):** that crash came from licensed manufacturers massively overprinting to meet speculative demand. Two 2026 signals rhyme with it: Pokémon printing at maximum capacity (about 10B/yr vs 1.5–2B pre-2019) and Fanatics consolidating all big-3 US sports licenses. Two differences: today's grading/pop-report infrastructure makes high-grade scarcity measurable, and the buyer base is much broader (international, adult, TCG).
- **For a solo operator:** buying modern sealed or modern singles for appreciation now has negative expected value near-term (20–50% drawdowns are already underway). Transaction-volume businesses (selling services, tools, grading prep, breaks, consignment, data) benefit from record volumes regardless of price direction, but are exposed if volumes roll over.

### Gaps
- No direct primary data was pulled from Card Ladder, Alt or PriceCharting index pages for Sept 2026; numbers come via secondary articles.
- No hard sourced figures were found for the 1990s junk-wax market size or crash magnitude.
- No 2022–2023 peak-to-trough % for the Card Ladder Pokémon index was found.

## 4. Platform volumes (eBay, Whatnot, TCGplayer, Fanatics Collect, Courtyard, Alt, Arena Club, PSA, Collectors)

### Takeaway
Every measurable platform is at record volume in 2025–26. Whatnot: $8B+ GMV in 2025, $8B+ again in H1 2026 alone, and a $20B valuation in Aug 2026. eBay: total GMV +14% YoY to $22.4B in Q2 2026, focus categories +26%, cards the #1 driver. PSA: 19.26M cards graded in 2025 (+26%). Industry-wide grading was 26.6M. Courtyard does about $20–30M/week. Card-specific GMV is not broken out by eBay, TCGplayer or Fanatics Collect.

### Cited Findings

## 5. Who the buyers are now

### Takeaway
Adults drive the market. In the US, 19% of adults bought Pokémon cards for themselves in a six-month window, and only about 25% of those play the game. Adults 18+ are the fastest-growing and highest-spending toy buyer group. Gen Z/younger buyers are coming in through Whatnot and live breaks. Europe is a major growth market for Pokémon. Retail scarcity has produced scalping, purchase limits and pre-dawn online drops.

### Cited Findings
- 19% of US adults bought Pokémon cards for themselves in the past 6 months, and only about one-quarter of them play the game. Others collect, display or bulk-buy to resell. 18+ toy spending was +12% in Q1 2025 to $1.8B — [Retail Brew/Circana, 2025-06-06](https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2025/06/06/collectible-trading-cards-and-adults-drive-toy-sales-in-2025-circana) [PRIMARY data]
- Whatnot is often the first marketplace younger collectors use, not eBay — [search summary of thecardshopfinder guide](https://thecardshopfinder.com/guides/selling-cards/) [WEAK]
- Demographic stats of weak provenance: 35% of US collectors aged 18–24; millennials (25–40) account for 55% of spending; average sports card collector age 37 (2023); 72% male globally (2023) — [Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/trading-cards-industry-statistics/); [pregradecards demographics](https://pregradecards.com/blog/card-market-demographics-study) [WEAK, aggregator; treat as directional only]
- **International:** Pokémon was the #1 toy property in the UK, Germany, Italy, France, Belgium and the Netherlands in 2025, with growth of +45% to +70% — [Mojo Nation (Circana)](https://www.mojo-nation.com/pokemon-enjoyed-year-of-massive-buzz-intense-demand-and-vigorous-sales-in-2025/). Whatnot says Europe is growing steadily — [SCD](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/whatnot-report-shows-sports-cards-remain-a-core-driver-as-live-commerce-expands-globally/). eBay extended card Authenticity Guarantee to the UK — [eBay Q2'26](https://ebay.q4cdn.com/610426115/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/2026_Q2_eBay_Earnings_Presentation_Final.pdf)
- **Retail scarcity and scalping:** 30th Celebration sold out in minutes on 2026-09-16. Target limits are 2 per SKU and it runs overnight (3–5 AM ET) restock drops tracked by restock-alert accounts — [Swisher Post](https://www.swisherpost.com/gaming/pokemon-30th-anniversary-target-restock-limits/); [Ricanking restock tracker on X](https://x.com/ricanking6/status/2100074034020884923) [WEAK/social]

### Inferences
- The buyer base is an adult nostalgia + speculation segment (Pokémon 90s kids now in their 30s–40s) layered on top of the play/kid segment. Because the speculator/reseller share is significant ("bulk to resell"), the market is sensitive to price momentum, which magnifies both the boom and any bust.

### Gaps
- No primary-source demographic survey (e.g., eBay, Whatnot or Collectors buyer data) with age breakdowns for 2025–26 was found.
- No hard data on China (the Chinese-language Pokémon/Riftbound market) or Japanese domestic market size was found. The Japan "turning point April 2026" piece was not verified.
